Conformal prediction has recently been proposed for the diagnosis of classifiers and the selection of federated clients, with the prediction sets themselves treated as data from which class similarity, confusion and client quality are read off. We give a specific caution in the form of a no-go theorem and a one-bit calculation. Over the class of joint laws constrained only by $\hat Y \in C(X)$ whenever $C(X) \ne \varnothing$, the law of the prediction sets does not identify the confusion matrix once a set of size at least two occurs, and the corresponding failure inside randomized adaptive prediction sets is exhibited by construction. We illustrate with the conformal correlation matrix of Perlo, Chiasserini, De Veciana and Malandrino (2026), the phi coefficient between set membership indicators, a natural construction that is likely to be reinvented. Two classifiers built inside randomized adaptive prediction sets, exactly as Romano, Sesia and Candes specify them, produce the same matrix while confusing every class in opposite directions. Seven further hand calculations give a zero entry beside 99% directional confusion, an entry of one beside one in a million co-occurrence, and an entry of $\pm 0.64$ from pooling two clients with zero correlation on each. For classes that never share a set the entry is a function of the inclusion rates alone, and for a perfect classifier with equal top probabilities inside randomized adaptive prediction sets the matrix reveals a client's class proportions exactly, which undoes the privacy claim made for it.
