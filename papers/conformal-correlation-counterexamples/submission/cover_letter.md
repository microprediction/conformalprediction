Dear Professor Turgut,

I am submitting "Conformal Correlation Counterexamples", a short note that answers a paper
published in Computer Communications: Perlo, Chiasserini, De Veciana and Malandrino,
"Characterizing the performance of classification models through conformal correlation matrices",
volume 247 (2026), 108398.

That paper proposes correlating the membership indicators of conformal prediction sets and reading
the result as a map of which classes a classifier confuses. The note shows, by construction inside
the same randomized adaptive prediction sets the paper uses, that two classifiers can produce the
same law of prediction sets and the same correlation matrix while confusing every class in opposite
directions, and proves a short theorem that no statistic of the prediction sets determines the
confusion matrix once a set of size two occurs. Seven further hand calculations show that the sign
and magnitude of an entry do not identify similarity, dissimilarity, independence or directional
confusion, and that the matrix reveals a client's class proportions exactly in a setting where the
paper claims it protects them. A script that recomputes every quantity accompanies the note.

The journal's Guide for Authors lists research and review articles, and the journal has published
comments on its own articles before, most recently in 2012. I leave the article type to your
judgement and have submitted under the closest available category.

Two authors of the paper hold editorial roles at the journal: Professor Chiasserini as Associate
Editor-in-Chief and Dr Malandrino on the Technical Committee. Under the journal's policy that
editors are not involved in decisions about papers relating to their own work, I ask that the
handling of this submission be kept independent of both.

The note is not under consideration elsewhere. A preprint is on arXiv. I have no competing
interests and no funding to declare, and I choose to publish under the subscription model.
