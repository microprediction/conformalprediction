\title{One Poisson Equation for Conformal Coverage under Dependence:\\
Exact State Correction and the Honesty Margin}
\author{Peter Cotton\\\texttt{peter.cotton@microprediction.com}}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Conformal prediction under dependent calibration data has two coupled defects. Dependence
deforms the law of the order-statistic threshold, and the current dynamical state shifts the
law of the future score. We show a single Poisson equation controls both. The realized
conditional coverage of the empirical-quantile threshold decomposes exactly into its
invariant-law value plus a current-state corrector, with no asymptotics. The same Poisson
operator supplies the long-run calibration variance, so state bias and effective-sample-size
loss are two faces of one fast eigenmode. A Freedman bound then yields a finite-sample,
state-conditionally honest quantile correction.
\end{abstract}

\section{The exact object}

Take a stationary time series of nonconformity scores and the ordinary split-conformal
threshold, the empirical $(1-\alpha)$-quantile of a calibration block. Two things are true
at once and are usually conflated. The calibration scores are dependent, so the order
statistic used as the threshold is not the one an i.i.d.\ analysis assumes. And the test
point is generated by a dynamical process whose current state is not the invariant state, so
the law of the score being covered is not the invariant law. The exact object of study is
neither the invariant law nor the marginal coverage. It is the realized conditional coverage
of a random order-statistic threshold, and both effects act on it.

It is tempting to say that ordinary conformal prediction ``calibrates the invariant law.''
Under dependence that is not an exact guarantee. Dependence alters the law of the order
statistic, and the current state alters the law of the future test score. The contribution
here is that a single Poisson equation governs both perturbations at once: the transient gap
between the current-state and invariant score laws, and the long-run calibration noise of the
dependent empirical quantile. The central identity, equation~\eqref{eq:corrector} below, is
exact and asymptotics-free, and we verify it numerically to machine precision.

\paragraph{Related work.} Two effects of this kind have been described before, and we treat
them as background rather than as the contribution. Transported-beta analyses separate a
test-side coverage transport from a deformation of the calibration order statistic, with the
Gaussian AR(1) case worked out explicitly. Markov distributional conformal prediction
constructs a conditional probability integral transform from estimated transition CDFs. What
the Poisson view adds is operational: the same operator that supplies the state corrector
also supplies a martingale representation of the calibration term and its long-run variance,
which is what the honesty margin needs. The nonparametric price of a fixed-point guarantee is
the subject of a companion note \citep{cotton2026honest}, whose local modulus reappears in
Section~\ref{sec:modulus}.

\section{The exact coverage decomposition}\label{sec:decomp}

Let $(W_t)$ be a Markov chain with transition kernel $P$ and invariant distribution $\pi$,
on a state space rich enough to absorb any emission randomness, so that the score is a
deterministic readout $R_t = r(W_t)$. (Replacing the indicator below by a conditional
emission CDF costs nothing.) For each threshold $q$ write the coverage function and the
invariant score CDF
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hdef}
  H_q(w) = \one\{r(w)\le q\},
  \qquad
  F(q) = \pi H_q .
\end{equation}
Let $R_{(k)}$ be the $k$th order statistic of the calibration scores $R_1,\dots,R_n$, and let
$\Fcal_n$ be the calibration information. If the test score is observed $\ell$ steps after the
calibration block, its realized conditional coverage is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Cdef}
  C_{n,\ell,k} = \Prob\{R_{n+\ell}\le R_{(k)}\given \Fcal_n\}.
\end{equation}
Conditional on the present state $W_n$, the Markov property gives
$C_{n,\ell,k} = P^{\ell} H_{R_{(k)}}(W_n)$, the $\ell$-step transported coverage function
evaluated at the current state.

The invariant law is the wrong reference by exactly a solved Poisson equation. Fix $q$ and
solve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson}
  (I-P)\chi_q = H_q - F(q),
  \qquad \pi\chi_q = 0 ,
\end{equation}
which has a unique centered solution under standard ergodicity \citep{meyntweedie2009}. Since
$F(q)$ is constant and $P^\ell$ fixes constants,
\[
  P^{\ell}H_q - F(q) = P^{\ell}(I-P)\chi_q = (P^{\ell}-P^{\ell+1})\chi_q .
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact conformal coverage corrector]\label{thm:corrector}
For every calibration threshold $R_{(k)}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrector}
  C_{n,\ell,k} = F(R_{(k)}) + (P^{\ell}-P^{\ell+1})\chi_{R_{(k)}}(W_n) .
\end{equation}
The first term is the coverage of the random calibration threshold under the invariant score
law. The second is the exact current-state correction. If the state is latent with filtering
distribution $\mu_n(\cdot)=\Prob(W_n\in\cdot\given\Fcal_n)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrector-latent}
  C_{n,\ell,k} = F(R_{(k)}) + \mu_n(P^{\ell}-P^{\ell+1})\chi_{R_{(k)}} .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

No asymptotics enter. The decomposition is an algebraic identity given the Poisson solution,
and it holds for every $n$, $\ell$, $k$. A direct simulation on a finite chain confirms both
sides of \eqref{eq:corrector} agree to machine precision.

\section{A transported order-statistic representation}\label{sec:transport}

Suppose $F$ is continuous and set $U_i = F(R_i)$. Each $U_i$ is marginally uniform under
stationarity, though the sequence need not be independent, and monotonicity of $F$ gives
$F(R_{(k)}) = U_{(k)}$. Define the state-dependent coverage transport
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau}
  \tau_{\ell,w}(u) = P^{\ell}H_{F^{-1}(u)}(w)
    = u + (P^{\ell}-P^{\ell+1})\chi_{F^{-1}(u)}(w) .
\end{equation}
Then Theorem~\ref{thm:corrector} reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau-C}
  C_{n,\ell,k} = \tau_{\ell,W_n}(U_{(k)}) ,
\end{equation}
which separates the problem cleanly. The law of $U_{(k)}$ carries the calibration dependence.
The map $\tau_{\ell,W_n}$ carries the test-side state dependence. Under i.i.d.\ calibration
$U_{(k)}$ has the familiar beta law and $\tau$ is the identity, recovering ordinary conformal
coverage. Under dependent calibration the law of $U_{(k)}$ is generally not beta, and its
leading variance is set by the long-run variance of the coverage indicator, computed in
Section~\ref{sec:variance}. This is the transported-beta separation, to which the Poisson
representation adds the explicit corrector and a martingale representation of the calibration
term.

\section{The first-order coverage expansion}\label{sec:expansion}

Fix $p=1-\alpha$ and $q_p = F^{-1}(p)$, and abbreviate the transient corrector
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bias}
  b_{\ell,w}(q) = P^{\ell}H_q(w) - F(q) = (P^{\ell}-P^{\ell+1})\chi_q(w) .
\end{equation}
The dependent empirical quantile obeys a Bahadur representation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bahadur}
  F(R_{(k_p)}) - p = -\big[F_n(q_p) - p\big] + o_p(n^{-1/2}),
  \qquad F_n(q) = \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \one\{R_i\le q\},
\end{equation}
valid under geometric ergodicity with $F$ differentiable at $q_p$, $f(q_p)>0$. Expanding
\eqref{eq:corrector} around $q_p$ and using \eqref{eq:bahadur},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expansion}
  C_{n,\ell,k_p} - p
  = b_{\ell,W_n}(q_p)
    - \Big[1 + \tfrac{1}{f(q_p)}\partial_q b_{\ell,W_n}(q_p)\Big]\big[F_n(q_p)-p\big]
    + o_p(n^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
When the transient correction is small in $C^1$, $\|b_{\ell,\cdot}\|_{C^1}=o(1)$, this reduces
to the central formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:central}
  C_{n,\ell,k_p} - p = b_{\ell,W_n}(q_p) - \big[F_n(q_p)-p\big]
    + o_p\big(\|b_{\ell,\cdot}\| + n^{-1/2}\big) .
\end{equation}
Realized coverage error has two first-order sources: a current-state bias
$b_{\ell,W_n}(q_p)$ and a calibration-CDF error $F_n(q_p)-p$. The first-order
probability-level correction is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:level-correction}
  p \;\longmapsto\; p - b_{\ell,W_n}(q_p) .
\end{equation}
If the current state causes undercoverage then $b_{\ell,W_n}(q_p)<0$ and the required
empirical quantile level rises. A one-sided honesty margin is then added on top, and its size
is set by the same operator, as we show next.

\section{The same Poisson equation gives the calibration variance}\label{sec:variance}

Center the coverage indicator at the invariant quantile,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g}
  g_p(w) = H_{q_p}(w) - p ,
\end{equation}
and solve a second Poisson equation with the same operator,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson2}
  (I-P)\psi_p = g_p, \qquad \pi\psi_p = 0 .
\end{equation}
The additive calibration error has the exact martingale decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:martingale}
  \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} g_p(W_i) = \psi_p(W_0) - \psi_p(W_n) + \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} D_{i+1},
  \qquad D_{i+1} = \psi_p(W_{i+1}) - P\psi_p(W_i) ,
\end{equation}
with $(D_{i+1})$ a martingale-difference sequence. Its long-run variance is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lrv}
  \sigma_p^2 = \pi\big[\psi_p^2 - (P\psi_p)^2\big]
    = p(1-p) + 2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Cov_\pi\big(\one\{R_0\le q_p\},\,\one\{R_j\le q_p\}\big) .
\end{equation}
So one operator supplies both the transient bias $b_{\ell,w}(q)=(P^\ell-P^{\ell+1})\chi_q(w)$
and the calibration variance $\sigma_p^2 = \pi[\psi_p^2-(P\psi_p)^2]$. These are not
unrelated corrections. They are two manifestations of the same unresolved fast dynamics.
The corrector $b_{\ell,w}$ measures how much fast-state information survives to the test
horizon. The variance $\sigma_p^2$ measures how much fast-state persistence removes
calibration information.

\section{A fast two-state volatility model}\label{sec:twostate}

The cleanest nontrivial instance is not AR(1) but a fast volatility regime. Let
$V^\varepsilon_t\in\{L,H\}$ be a continuous-time chain with generator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generator}
  Q_\varepsilon = \frac1\varepsilon\begin{pmatrix} -a & a\\ b & -b\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad \kappa = a+b, \quad \pi_H = \frac{a}{a+b},\quad \pi_L=\frac{b}{a+b}.
\end{equation}
Conditional on the state, the score CDFs are $F_L(q)$ and $F_H(q)$, and the invariant score
law is the mixture $F(q) = \pi_L F_L(q) + \pi_H F_H(q)$. If the filtered high-state
probability at time zero is $\omega_0 = \Prob(V_0=H\given\Fcal_0)$, then at horizon $t$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omega}
  \omega_t = \pi_H + e^{-\kappa t/\varepsilon}(\omega_0 - \pi_H) ,
\end{equation}
so the exact conditional score CDF is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gt}
  G^\varepsilon_t(q) = F(q) + e^{-\kappa t/\varepsilon}(\omega_0-\pi_H)\big[F_H(q)-F_L(q)\big],
\end{equation}
and the transient coverage error is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary}
  b^\varepsilon_t(q) = e^{-\kappa t/\varepsilon}(\omega_0-\pi_H)\big[F_H(q)-F_L(q)\big] .
\end{equation}
This is a literal homogenization boundary layer. After several fast mixing times it is
exponentially small. At a forecast horizon comparable to $\varepsilon$ it is not small at
all. To first order the quantile correction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qcorr}
  q^\varepsilon_{t,p} = q_p
    - \frac{e^{-\kappa t/\varepsilon}(\omega_0-\pi_H)\big[F_H(q_p)-F_L(q_p)\big]}{f(q_p)}
    + O\big(e^{-2\kappa t/\varepsilon}\big),
  \quad f = \pi_L f_L + \pi_H f_H .
\end{equation}
For a nonnegative absolute-error score the high-volatility state usually has $F_H(q)<F_L(q)$
at the relevant $q$. If the current high-volatility probability exceeds stationary,
$\omega_0>\pi_H$, then $b^\varepsilon_t(q_p)<0$ and \eqref{eq:qcorr} raises the threshold, as
it should.

\paragraph{Discrete sampling and calibration dependence.} Sample every $\Delta$ time units.
The nontrivial eigenvalue of the sampled transition matrix is $\lambda = e^{-\kappa\Delta/
\varepsilon}$. Writing $d_p = F_H(q_p)-F_L(q_p)$, the coverage indicator
$I_j=\one\{R_j\le q_p\}$ has autocovariance $\Cov(I_0,I_j) = \pi_L\pi_H d_p^2\lambda^{j}$ for
$j\ge1$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sig2-twostate}
  \sigma_p^2 = p(1-p) + 2\pi_L\pi_H d_p^2\,\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda} .
\end{equation}
The same quantities control both problems,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:both}
  \underbrace{b^\varepsilon_{m}(q_p) \propto \lambda^{m}(\omega_0-\pi_H)d_p}_{\text{transient bias}},
  \qquad
  \underbrace{\sigma_p^2 - p(1-p) \propto \pi_L\pi_H d_p^2\tfrac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}}_{\text{calibration variance inflation}} .
\end{equation}
One fast eigenmode produces both conditional miscoverage and effective-sample-size loss. When
$\Delta\ll\varepsilon$ we have $1-\lambda\sim\kappa\Delta/\varepsilon$, so
$\lambda/(1-\lambda)\sim\varepsilon/(\kappa\Delta)$. Sampling faster then piles up redundant
calibration scores, and for a window of fixed physical duration $T=n\Delta$ the effective
sample size saturates at order $T/\varepsilon$ rather than growing like $n$.

\paragraph{A numerical illustration.} Take $p=0.9$, $\pi_H=0.2$, $\lambda=0.8$, and at the
invariant $0.9$-quantile $F_L(q_p)=0.95$, $F_H(q_p)=0.70$, so the mixture is
$0.8(0.95)+0.2(0.70)=0.90$. If the current state is high volatility the one-step coverage of
the invariant threshold is $0.9+0.8(1-0.2)(0.70-0.95)=0.74$. If it is low volatility it is
$0.9+0.8(0-0.2)(0.70-0.95)=0.94$. The same pooled threshold gives either $74\%$ or $94\%$
conditional coverage while appearing perfectly calibrated at $90\%$ in stationarity. The
long-run variance is $\sigma_p^2 = 0.09 + 2(0.8)(0.2)(0.25)^2(0.8/0.2) = 0.17$, an integrated
dependence factor $\sigma_p^2/[p(1-p)] = 0.17/0.09 \approx 1.89$, so the nominal calibration
sample behaves like only $\approx 53\%$ as many independent observations. A direct simulation
of the sampled chain reproduces $\sigma_p^2$. The persistent regime creates severe
current-state undercoverage and nearly halves the effective calibration sample at the same
time.

\section{A finite-sample honest Markov correction}\label{sec:honest}

The expansion motivates an actual finite-sample theorem. For horizon $\ell$ define the worst
adverse transient discrepancy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:al}
  a_\ell = \sup_{w,q}\big[F(q) - P^{\ell}H_q(w)\big]_+ ,
  \qquad\text{so}\qquad P^{\ell}H_q(w)\ge F(q) - a_\ell
\end{equation}
for every state and threshold. Choose the invariant target level $u = p + a_\ell$, set
$q_u=F^{-1}(u)$ and $g_u(w) = H_{q_u}(w) - u$, and solve $(I-P)\psi_u = g_u$. Assume the
uniform bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BV}
  \|\psi_u\|_\infty \le B,
  \qquad
  P\psi_u^2(w) - (P\psi_u(w))^2 \le V \quad\text{uniformly in } w .
\end{equation}
With $x=\log(1/\eta)$ define the deviation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta}
  \delta_{n,\eta} = \frac{2B + \sqrt{2nVx}}{n} + \frac{2Bx}{3n} ,
\end{equation}
and set $k = \lfloor n(u+\delta_{n,\eta})\rfloor + 1$, with $R_{(k)}=+\infty$ if $k>n$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-sample honest Markov correction]\label{thm:honest}
Under the preceding assumptions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:honest-guarantee}
  \Prob\Big\{\, \Prob\big(R_{n+\ell}\le R_{(k)}\given\Fcal_n\big) \ge p \,\Big\} \ge 1-\eta .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the martingale decomposition \eqref{eq:martingale} applied at $q_u$,
$\sum_{i=1}^n[\one\{R_i\le q_u\}-u]$ equals a martingale plus endpoint terms bounded by $2B$.
Freedman's inequality \citep{freedman1975} with the variance bound \eqref{eq:BV} gives, with
probability at least $1-\eta$,
\[
  F_n(q_u) \le u + \delta_{n,\eta} .
\]
On this event fewer than $k$ calibration scores lie at or below $q_u$, since $nF_n(q_u) \le
n(u+\delta_{n,\eta}) < k$, so $R_{(k)} \ge q_u$ and hence $F(R_{(k)}) \ge u = p + a_\ell$.
By the definition of $a_\ell$,
\[
  \Prob\big(R_{n+\ell}\le R_{(k)}\given\Fcal_n\big) = P^{\ell}H_{R_{(k)}}(W_n)
    \ge F(R_{(k)}) - a_\ell \ge p ,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:honest-guarantee}.
\end{proof}

This is a genuine calibration-sample honest and state-conditionally valid guarantee, stronger
than a statement about average marginal coverage. If $B$, $V$, and $a_\ell$ hold uniformly
over a dynamic model class, the guarantee is uniform over that class.

\section{Interpretation}\label{sec:interp}

The corrected empirical quantile level is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected-level}
  p + \underbrace{a_\ell}_{\text{fast-state protection}}
    + \underbrace{\delta_{n,\eta}}_{\text{dependent-calibration protection}} ,
\end{equation}
and in threshold units the first-order inflation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threshold-inflation}
  T_n - q_p \approx \frac{a_\ell + \delta_{n,\eta}}{f(q_p)} .
\end{equation}
The two costs are qualitatively different. The transient term $a_\ell$ is not an estimation
error. It is the real difference between the current conditional law and the invariant law.
The term $\delta_{n,\eta}$ is the price of demanding that the realized calibration sample be
trustworthy with probability $1-\eta$. A state-adaptive implementation need not pay the
worst-case $a_\ell$. It can estimate $b_{\ell,W_n}(q)=(P^\ell-P^{\ell+1})\chi_q(W_n)$ and
build a lower confidence envelope for the current conditional CDF. Theorem~\ref{thm:honest}
is the simplest version with a transparent proof.

\section{Combining with the local sharpness modulus}\label{sec:modulus}

The companion note \citep{cotton2026honest} asks for a stronger guarantee: with high
probability over calibration data, the realized threshold must cover the conditional score
law at a fixed local covariate value $x_0$. It proves that this forces inflation at the local
nonparametric modulus $(n g(x_0))^{-s/(2s+d)}$, from locally indistinguishable alternatives
rather than any exchangeability phenomenon. With dependent fast--slow scores the natural
upper bound becomes, up to fixed regularity constants,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hierarchy-bound}
  T_{n,h}(x_0) - q_p(x_0) \lesssim
    C_q L h^{s}
    + \frac{\sigma_{p,x_0}\sqrt{\log(1/\eta)}}{f_{p,x_0}\sqrt{n g(x_0) h^{d}}}
    + \frac{a_{\ell,x_0}}{f_{p,x_0}}
    + R_{\mathrm{hom}}(\varepsilon) + \Delta_{\mathrm{dyn}} .
\end{equation}
The terms are local smoothing bias $C_q L h^s$, dependent local calibration noise through the
long-run variance $\sigma_{p,x_0}$, remaining fast-state memory $a_{\ell,x_0}$, the
homogenization approximation error $R_{\mathrm{hom}}(\varepsilon)$, and the cost
$\Delta_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ of estimating the transition dynamics, filter, and cell problem.
Minimizing the first two terms over $h$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hstar}
  h_\star \asymp \Big[\frac{\sigma_{p,x_0}^2}{n g(x_0) L^2}\Big]^{1/(2s+d)},
  \qquad
  \text{local inflation} \asymp L^{d/(2s+d)}\Big[\frac{\sigma_{p,x_0}^2}{n g(x_0)}\Big]^{s/(2s+d)} .
\end{equation}
Dependence changes the information constant through the long-run variance but leaves the
H\"older exponent unchanged. Homogenization adds the transient and dependence terms and does
not erase the local modulus. The result is a clean hierarchy,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hierarchy}
  \begin{aligned}
    \text{local heterogeneity} &\;\to\; \text{nonparametric modulus},\\
    \text{calibration persistence} &\;\to\; \text{Poisson long-run variance},\\
    \text{current fast state} &\;\to\; \text{Poisson transient corrector},\\
    \text{approximating the fast--slow model} &\;\to\; \text{homogenization remainder}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first term is unavoidable over a broad H\"older class. The second and third can be
quantified and corrected under dynamical structure. The fourth must be included for the
guarantee to be honest.

\section{What homogenization does and does not buy}\label{sec:whatbuys}

This settles the conceptual question. Homogenization does not defeat the sharpness lower
bound. It partitions the conformal error into unavoidable local ambiguity, correctable
dynamical bias, and estimable dependence noise. If the fast--slow dynamics live in a
finite-dimensional parametric family, honest uncertainty about the dynamical correction
shrinks at the parametric rate $n^{-1/2}$ rather than the nonparametric local rate
$n^{-s/(2s+d)}$. That gain does not contradict the companion note. It comes from replacing a
broad H\"older class by a much smaller structured experiment. If the transition law, the
state-specific score distributions, or the scale field are again allowed to vary
nonparametrically with $x$, the local modulus returns, and if their smoothness is unknown the
no-adaptation obstruction returns with it.

\section{The corresponding skaters transform}\label{sec:skaters}

The correction is directly usable. For an existing distributional forecast $F^0_{t+1}$,
compute the Gaussianized probability integral transform $Z_t = \Phi^{-1}(F^0_t(Y_t))$, and
fit a predictable two-state model to the past $Z_t$ with state-specific CDFs $K_L,K_H$ and
filtered high-state probability $\omega_t$. The one-step forecast is $\omega_{t+1\mid t} =
\pi_H + \lambda(\omega_t-\pi_H)$, so the conditional CDF of the next Gaussianized PIT is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Kpred}
  K_{t+1\mid t}(z) = (1-\omega_{t+1\mid t})K_L(z) + \omega_{t+1\mid t}K_H(z) ,
\end{equation}
and the dynamically corrected predictive CDF and $u$-quantile are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected-cdf}
  F_{t+1}(y) = K_{t+1\mid t}\big[\Phi^{-1}(F^0_{t+1}(y))\big],
  \qquad
  \widehat Q_{t+1}(u) = (F^0_{t+1})^{-1}\big[\Phi(K^{-1}_{t+1\mid t}(u))\big] .
\end{equation}
This is a direct replacement for the marginal fence-post transform. The fence-post map
estimates a pooled PIT law. Equation~\eqref{eq:corrected-cdf} estimates the conditional PIT
law after accounting for the current residual regime. For an honest upper quantile, let
$\mathcal{C}_t$ be a confidence set for the dynamic parameters and add a homogenization or
misspecification radius $r_t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:honest-skater}
  z^{\mathrm{hon}}_{t+1,p} = \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{C}_t} K^{-1}_{\theta,t+1\mid t}(p+r_t),
  \qquad
  y^{\mathrm{hon}}_{t+1,p} = (F^0_{t+1})^{-1}\big[\Phi(z^{\mathrm{hon}}_{t+1,p})\big] .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Empirical protocol.} A comparison should include the base forecast, the marginal
fence-post correction, an AR correction, the two-state homogenized correction, and the honest
two-state correction. The decisive diagnostics are PIT calibration conditional on the
filtered state, coverage conditional on recent $Z_t$ and volatility, the lower-tail
probability of realized coverage, proper scores such as log score and CRPS, and the extra
interval width the honest envelope demands. Aggregate PIT uniformity alone is insufficient.
The two-state example shows that pooled $90\%$ calibration can conceal conditional coverage
ranging from $74\%$ to $94\%$.

\section{Conclusion}

The conformal coverage process under Markov dependence admits an exact invariant-plus-Poisson
decomposition, equation~\eqref{eq:corrector}, and the same Poisson equation determines both
the current-state coverage bias and the dependence-adjusted honesty margin. State bias and
effective-sample-size loss are two readings of one fast eigenmode. Placed next to the local
sharpness modulus, this identifies precisely what dynamical structure can and cannot buy back:
it corrects the transient and dependence terms, it does not remove the nonparametric floor,
and honest use requires paying for the approximation of the fast--slow model itself.
