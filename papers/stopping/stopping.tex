\title{The Price of Eschewing Smoothness and Stopping Early:\\
a Theoretical and Empirical Analysis of Conformal Methods}
\author{Peter Cotton\\\texttt{peter.cotton@microprediction.com}}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Conformal practice makes two choices so uniformly that they pass for definitions:
residuals are pooled rather than smoothed, and the calibration step ends the chain.
Of 686 arXiv papers titled with conformal prediction, 94.5\% never depart from
pooling at any granularity and 87\% stop at the empirical map. We price the two
choices separately, on 212 economic series against a strong online forecaster.
Pooling costs 0.14 nats and buys the guarantee; a separation theorem shows local
smoothing cannot lower that price without forfeiting exactness. Stopping costs a
further 0.44 nats and buys nothing. Coverage detects neither.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A conformal prediction system is assembled in a fixed order: fit a model, compute
residuals on held-out data, pool their empirical law, read the answer off the
pooled quantiles. Two conventions hide in that sentence. The residual law is
\emph{pooled}: one shape serves every state, however much the truth varies with the
input. And the empirical map is \emph{terminal}: nothing is fitted to its output.
Neither convention is argued for in the papers that adopt it. This paper measures
what each costs, and separates the price that buys the coverage certificate from
the price that buys nothing.

The paper has exactly four components, and each subsequent section is one of them.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The count} (Section~\ref{sec:count}, empirical). In a frame of 686
  arXiv papers, 599 stop at the empirical map and 648 never depart from residual
  pooling at any granularity. One paper composes a model after the map. Every
  smooth-weighting paper that claims exactness was read in full; none overclaims,
  and the escapes they use are exactly the three the theory permits.
\item \textbf{The floor} (Section~\ref{sec:floor}, a proposition). The excess
  logarithmic risk of any frozen conformal scheme equals a conditional mutual
  information fixed by its score and stratification. No calibration choice moves
  it. This is classical in spirit and stated because Section~\ref{sec:price} uses
  it.
\item \textbf{The separation theorem} (Section~\ref{sec:smooth}, the theoretical
  centre). Exact distribution-free validity forces uniform pooling of the
  calibration residuals; test-centred smoothing opens a coverage gap of the same
  order as its bias, and the conflict is greatest exactly where smoothing would
  help most, at small samples. So the floor of Section~\ref{sec:floor} cannot be
  undercut from within.
\item \textbf{The price} (Section~\ref{sec:price}, empirical). Holding one strong
  forecaster and one empirical object fixed and moving only the object's
  \emph{position} in the chain: pooling costs $0.14$ nats net of estimation
  effects, while stopping costs a further $0.44$, three times more. The sign of
  the CRPS comparison flips across the same line. Adaptivity in time recovers
  $0.075$ of it. Coverage registers none of it.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:discussion} states the recommendation, which is not a better
calibrator: keep the map, keep the certificate where it is wanted, and keep
modelling past it.

\paragraph{What is new and what is borrowed.} The count, the two results, and the
price are this paper's; both results are proved here, the floor in
Appendix~\ref{app:floor} and the separation in Appendix~\ref{app:sep}. The key
coverage inequality behind the separation is due to \citet{barber2023beyond}. The
impossibility of exact distribution-free conditional coverage is
\citet{lei2014distribution} and \citet{barber2021limits}; the inflation forced by
training-conditional coverage at a fixed point is classical
\citep{li1989honest,low1997nonparametric,cai2004adaptation}. The base forecaster
is the subject of \citet{cotton2026transforms}.

\section{The count}\label{sec:count}

This section establishes that the two conventions are near-universal. It is a
claim about published work, so it gets a protocol rather than a citation.

\subsection{Frame and classification}

The frame is every arXiv paper with ``conformal prediction'' in the title at
harvest date, $n = 686$. Each paper is classified on two orthogonal axes by
documented keyword screens over titles and abstracts, with the cells that matter
verified by reading; classifier, frame, and per-paper verdicts are in the
repository.

\emph{The position axis} records what a paper does to the fence-post empirical map
$E_n$: quote its output as the answer (\textsc{stop}); substitute a conditional
estimate for the pooled quantile (\textsc{replace}); keep the map and adapt the
level online (\textsc{tune}); feed its output to a downstream decision
(\textsc{consume}); or keep the map \emph{and fit a model on its output}
(\textsc{compose}), the only move that refutes the stopping claim.

\emph{The pooling axis} records whether the weights on calibration residuals ever
depend on the test point. \textsc{pooled} means one empirical law;
\textsc{stratified} means pooling within a pre-committed partition (Mondrian,
binned, group-conditional), which coarsens the granularity but still pools;
\textsc{smooth} means test-centred weighting (localized, kernel, similarity
weights on the calibration residuals). A smooth \emph{score} is not smooth
pooling: a kernel or normalized score is a richer score map, and the shape it
reshapes is still one pooled object. The first version of the screen missed this
distinction and was corrected; the correction is documented in the classifier.

axes are close to independent except in one corner: \textsc{replace} and
\textsc{smooth} nearly coincide ($14$ of $16$), as they should, since substituting
a conditional estimate is the smooth move. Twenty-one papers weight smoothly and
still stop, paying both of the title's prices at once.

\subsection{The nine reads}\label{sec:reads}

Nine \textsc{smooth} papers also claim exact or distribution-free validity in the
abstract. Under the separation theorem of Section~\ref{sec:smooth} each must be
using one of three escapes, so all nine were read in full. None overclaims. Four
restore \emph{marginal} validity by recalibration or design
\citep{guan2023localized,hore2023randomly}; one states a coverage bound with an
explicit gap in the style of \citet{barber2023beyond}; one uses known
covariate-shift tilts \citep{tibshirani2019conformal}; one proves finite-sample
\emph{local} bounds by assuming a smoothness class outright, surrendering
distribution-freeness. Two were screen false positives whose smoothing lives in
the score, and were reclassified \textsc{pooled}. \citet{hore2023randomly} state
the boundary in the community's own words: for local coverage, ``there are no
known distribution-free theoretical results to guarantee this.''

The field's best localization papers therefore already respect the line the
theorem draws. What the theorem adds is why the line is where it is, and
Section~\ref{sec:price} adds what crossing it, or declining to, is worth.

\subsection{Refutation criteria}

The stopping claim is falsified by a paper that retains the empirical rank
transform, applies it to residuals, and fits a downstream model on the transformed
stream, keeping the transform in the chain. Reweighting the empirical law does not
count; conditioning upstream does not count; adapting the level does not count.
The pooling claim is falsified by exhibiting exact distribution-free validity with
test-centred weights outside the known covariate-shift escape. Outside the
conformal banner the composing move is routine: the normal quantile transform and
meta-Gaussian forecasting \citep{bogner2012}, copula time series
\citep{chenfan2006}, and autoregressive transformation models \citep{rugamer2023}
all apply an empirical marginal map and keep modelling in the transformed
coordinates. The absence is a fact about a community's habit, not about the
difficulty of the idea.

\section{The floor}\label{sec:floor}

This section states the identity that Section~\ref{sec:price} measures against.
It is borderline classical and is presented as a proposition with lineage, not as
a discovery.

Fix a nonconformity score $A:\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$, strictly
increasing in its second argument, and a stratification
$\kappa:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{K}$, both frozen independently of the calibration
labels. Every split, normalized, Mondrian, quantile, and predictive-system
conformal scheme emits the same object: the pooled empirical law of the scores in
the test point's stratum, reshaped through $A(x,\cdot)^{-1}$; the named schemes
differ only in $(A,\kappa)$. Write $W=A(X,Y)$.

\begin{proposition}[Irreducible floor]\label{prop:floor}
Suppose $(A,\kappa)$ is frozen independently of the calibration labels, each
$A(x,\cdot)$ is a strictly increasing absolutely continuous bijection, and the
relevant densities and divergences exist and are finite. Then the
excess logarithmic risk of the conformal predictor over the conditional oracle
satisfies
\[
L(\widehat F_n) - H(Y\given X) \;=\; \I(W;X\given\kappa) \;+\; \Delta_n,
\qquad \Delta_n \to 0,
\]
where $\Delta_n$ is the pooled-density estimation term. Consequently no choice of
calibration estimator, quantile convention, randomization, or level reduces the
excess below $\I(W;X\given\kappa)$; only a change to $(A,\kappa)$ can.
\end{proposition}

The proof is a change of variables in which the Jacobian of $A$ cancels between
truth and forecast, followed by the definition of conditional mutual information;
it is reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:floor}. With a location score and one
stratum the floor is the plain residual information gap $\I(R;X)$.
Level adaptation \citep{gibbs2021adaptive} and fold averaging act on the pooled
scores alone, so they move $\Delta_n$ and nothing else.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:usecase}
The floor is zero exactly when $(A,\kappa)$ renders the score conditionally
independent of the input, that is, when the modelling was finished before the
conformal step. Conformal calibration is deployed precisely when one is unwilling
to assume that. The method's stated reason for existing is its lower bound.
\end{remark}

\section{The separation theorem}\label{sec:smooth}

This section is the theoretical centre. It shows the floor of
Proposition~\ref{prop:floor} cannot be undercut from within: the one estimator
that would lower it, local smoothing of the calibration residuals, is exactly the
estimator that exact validity forbids.

Assume the conditional residual law $\mu_x$ is H\"older-$s$ in total variation and
is not input-free. The efficient estimator of $\mu_{x_0}$ at effective sample size
$m$ is kernel-weighted with bandwidth $h^{*}(m)\asymp m^{-1/(2s+d)}$, wide when
$m$ is small. The coverage of a weighted conformal set obeys the bound of
\citet{barber2023beyond}: localization costs, in coverage, the weighted
total-variation distance between calibration and test residual laws, which under
the H\"older condition is of order $h^{s}$.

\begin{theorem}[Small-sample separation]
\label{thm:sep}
No weighted residual predictor attains both exact distribution-free coverage for
every law in the class and the minimax estimation rate for $\mu_{x_0}$, unless the
sample is unbounded or the residual law is input-free. Exact validity forces the
weights uniform within a pre-committed stratification; the estimation-optimal
bandwidth incurs a coverage gap of order $h^{*}(m)^{s}$; and the product of the two
deficits is bounded away from zero at every finite $m$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[The scarce-data regime]\label{cor:scarce}
The separation is maximal as $m\to 0$: in the regime where local smoothing most
improves the estimate, the exactly-valid predictor pays its full heterogeneity
constant.
\end{corollary}

Three consequences frame the measurement. First, \textsc{stratified} practice in
Table~\ref{tab:crosstab} is rational: pooling within pre-committed strata is what
the theorem permits, and it is how granularity can be bought without losing
exactness. Second, the \textsc{smooth} escapes found in Section~\ref{sec:reads}
are the theorem's own taxonomy realised: retreat to marginal validity, state the
gap, assume the class, or move the smoothness into the score. Third, the known
escapes are bounded. Fixed covariate-shift tilts \citep{tibshirani2019conformal}
evade the uniform-pooling conclusion because their weights do not depend on the
residual laws; refining the stratification toward sufficiency is permitted but,
because the labels may not steer the refinement, delivers a label-blind
regressogram, minimax-suboptimal wherever adaptation would help
\citep{vovk2022universal,stone1982optimal}. Coverage at a fixed point under a
training-conditional guarantee inflates by the local modulus for classical
reasons \citep{li1989honest,low1997nonparametric}, an orthogonal axis not used
here.

What no result in this section touches is \emph{position}. The theorem constrains
how the pooled law may be estimated. It says nothing about whether the chain may
continue past it, because position is not a coverage question. That is the gap the
next section measures.

\section{The price}\label{sec:price}

This section holds one forecaster and one empirical object fixed and moves only
the object's position in the chain. The design answers one question: of the
information a conformal-shaped step discards, how much is the floor of
Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}, which Theorem~\ref{thm:sep} protects, and how much
is the stopping convention of Section~\ref{sec:count}, which nothing protects?

\subsection{Design}\label{sec:design}

The base forecaster is \texttt{laplace}, an online likelihood-weighted pool of
roughly sixty candidate transform chains with multi-scale mixing, a generalized
Pareto tail splice, and online PIT resolution; it is the strongest arm in the
benchmark this study extends. The empirical object is a 41-node quantile grid of
the base model's own recent $h$-step residuals, kernel-smoothed at the Silverman
bandwidth, the same construction used by the AutoARIMA-plus-conformal baseline in
the public benchmark. Two arms place it:

\begin{description}
\item[stop.] The conformal convention. Take the finished predictive, keep its
  location, replace its shape with the pooled empirical law. Nothing acts
  downstream. This is a frozen conformal scheme with a location score and one
  stratum, so Proposition~\ref{prop:floor} applies to it with floor $\I(R;X)$.
\item[compose-after.] The same object installed as the terminal residual law
  \emph{inside} the chain: the candidate pool still weights it, the tail splice
  still repairs its extremes, PIT resolution still runs, and the transform chain
  still inverts it to the observation coordinate. This arm is not a conformal
  scheme; its design is not frozen and it carries no certificate. It is the one
  move Proposition~\ref{prop:floor} permits, a change of representation, executed
  after the map instead of before it.
\end{description}

Windows of 250, 400, and 750 residuals are all reported. Recency-weighted
variants (exponential weights, half-life 150) run in both positions, so no
comparison can be attributed to staleness; by Theorem~\ref{thm:sep} the weighted
variants have already surrendered exactness, which sharpens
Section~\ref{sec:adaptive}. The corpus is 212 FRED change series with at least
1{,}100 observations, context 1{,}024, twelve rolling origins per series, horizons
1, 6, and 12. Long contexts are necessary: at context 256 the three windows
coincide and the arms collapse into one model with three names. Differences carry
paired bootstrap intervals clustered by series, and win/draw/loss counts come from
a per-series Diebold--Mariano test. A control corpus of iid series in three laws
(Gaussian, $t_3$, Laplace) runs first: there pooling is correct, and a constrained
arm should lose nothing.

The raw deficits at $h=1$ are $0.846$ nats for stop (window 400; $1.087$ and

the same arms lose $0.267$ and $0.127$: about a third of each raw gap is the cost
of estimating 41 empirical quantiles rather than a parametric scale, and it is
subtracted in Table~\ref{tab:decomp}. The control behaves: a state-correcting
reference arm loses nothing there. At $h=6$ the position term is $0.47$ nats.

The theory mapping is asymmetric by construction. The stop arm is a genuine frozen
conformal object, so its floor is $\I(R;X)$ plus estimation. The compose-after arm
is the permitted move, and its $0.14$ is the residual gap of a far richer
representation. The $0.44$ between them is therefore a measured lower bound on how
much of $\I(R;X)$ a richer representation recovers: three quarters of the stop
arm's information loss was never floor at all. No theorem defends paying it, and
by Table~\ref{tab:crosstab}, $599$ of $686$ papers pay it.

\subsection{Adaptivity, priced}\label{sec:adaptive}

Recency weighting moves the stop arm from $-0.846$ to $-0.771$: adaptivity in time
recovers $0.075$ nats where position recovers $0.58$. Adaptive weighting tracks
the level of volatility through time and still quotes one shape per step
regardless of state; the state-dependent version that would close the gap is what
Theorem~\ref{thm:sep} prices. Level adaptation is dispatched by
Proposition~\ref{prop:floor} directly, since it acts on the pooled scores alone.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The two conventions of the title now have separate prices and separate statuses.
Eschewing smoothness costs $0.14$ nats here. It is protected: Theorem~\ref{thm:sep}
shows the exact guarantee cannot be kept without it, and the nine reads of
Section~\ref{sec:reads} show the field's own localization literature already
respects that boundary. Stopping early costs $0.44$ nats here, three times more.
It is protected by nothing, defended nowhere, and practiced in $87\%$ of the
frame.

The recommendation is therefore not a better calibrator, a smarter weighting, or a
tuned level; Proposition~\ref{prop:floor} closes that entire menu. It is to treat
the empirical map as what it provably is, a coordinate change with a certificate
attached at its position, and to keep modelling on its output. The compose-after
arm is one constructive existence proof, and it is conformal prediction with the
modelling finished, in the sense of Remark~\ref{rem:usecase} run in reverse: the
certificate is what one holds when the modelling is not finished, and the PIT
stream of the composed chain measures, rather than assumes, whether it ever was.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The count is a keyword screen with full-text verification
of the cells that carry the argument: the smooth-with-exact-claim cell is fully
read, the compose shortlist is fully read, and the 599-paper \textsc{stop} default
rests on the screen plus a documented refutation criterion. Every price is
measured on one base forecaster, one economic corpus, and one iid control; the
position effect should be replicated on a second base before the constants are
quoted as more than one instance. The compose-after arm carries no coverage
guarantee, which is the point being priced, and it must not be read as a proposed
better conformal method.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}}\label{app:floor}

Fix the fold, so $(A,\kappa)$ is deterministic. Given $X=x$, the true density of
$Y$ is $p(y\given x) = p_W(A(x,y)\given x)\,A'(x,y)$ by change of variables, and
the conformal density is $\widehat f_x(y) = \widehat
g_{\kappa(x)}(A(x,y))\,A'(x,y)$. The Jacobian cancels in the log ratio, so
$\KLop(P(\cdot\given x)\,\|\,\widehat F_x) = \KLop(P_W(\cdot\given
x)\,\|\,\widehat G_{\kappa(x)})$. Splitting at the population pooled law
$G_{\kappa(x)}$ and averaging over $X$ gives
$\E_X\,\KLop(P_W(\cdot\given X)\,\|\,P_W(\cdot\given\kappa(X))) + \Delta_n$, and
the first term is $\I(W;X\given\kappa)$ by definition. The floor depends only on
$(A,\kappa)$; every calibration operation is a function of the pooled scores and
so moves only $\Delta_n$. \hfill$\square$

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sep}}\label{app:sep}

Write the weighted estimator $\widehat\mu_w(x_0)=\sum_i w_i(x_0)\,\delta_{R_i}$
with kernel weights of bandwidth $h$. Two displays give the two horns.

\emph{Localization opens a coverage gap.} By the coverage bound for
non-exchangeable weighted conformal prediction \citep{barber2023beyond}, the
level-$(1-\alpha)$ set of the weighted law satisfies
\[
\Pr\{R_0\in C_\alpha(x_0)\}\;\ge\;1-\alpha-\sum_i \widetilde w_i(x_0)\,
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu_{X_i},\mu_{x_0}),
\]
attained in the worst case over laws with the stated total variations. Under the
H\"older-$s$ condition the weighted sum is
$\le C\sum_i\widetilde w_i\,\rho(x_0,X_i)^s=\Theta(h^s)$, since kernel weights
concentrate on $\{\rho\lesssim h\}$.

\emph{Exact validity forces uniform pooling.} Distribution-free validity for every
law in the class forces the localization term to zero. It is a weighted average of
nonnegative total variations, some strictly positive under heterogeneity, so the
weights must place no differential mass across covariate-distinct calibration
points: uniform pooling within a partition committed before $x_0$ is seen, the
$h\to\infty$ endpoint.

\emph{The separation.} Uniform pooling has total-variation bias equal to the
heterogeneity constant of the pooled region, positive and independent of $m$,
violating the minimax rate $m^{-s/(2s+d)}$, which tends to zero. Conversely any
estimator achieving that rate uses $h\asymp h^*(m)=m^{-1/(2s+d)}$ and by the first
display has coverage gap $\Theta(h^*(m)^s)=\Theta(m^{-s/(2s+d)})$, positive at
every finite $m$. Bias and coverage gap are both $\Theta(h^s)$ along the bandwidth
family, so the product of the two deficits is bounded away from zero for finite
$m$ and vanishes only as $m\to\infty$. The corollary is the monotonicity of
$h^*(m)$ in $m$. \hfill$\square$
