\title{Conformal Prediction is Early Stopping}
\author{Peter Cotton\\\texttt{peter.cotton@microprediction.com}}
\date{August 2026 (draft)}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Split conformal prediction ends where its calibration begins: fit a model, pool
its held-out residuals, quote the pooled quantiles, stop. Of 686 arXiv papers
with conformal prediction in the title, 599 stop at that empirical map, and the
one candidate for continuing past it, on reading, also stops. We show the stop
is the expensive part. An identity pins the irreducible excess log score of a
frozen scheme to its score and stratification; on 17{,}364 economic series,
stopping costs 0.40 to 0.48 nats after a null-control adjustment, composing
past the map recovers a third to a half, and empirical coverage
detects none of it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Conformal prediction attaches a guarantee to any point forecaster by a recipe.
Fit a model however you like. On $n$ held-out points, compute a scalar error
for each, for instance the absolute residual. Sort the errors, take the
$\lceil (n+1)\,0.9\rceil$-th smallest, and quote the model's prediction plus or
minus that value. If the held-out data and the future point are exchangeable,
meaning their joint law is unchanged by shuffling, the quoted interval contains
the truth at least 90\% of the time, at any sample size, with no assumption on
the model or the data distribution.

The recipe ends with the percentile. Nothing is ever fitted to the output of the
pooled-error step: the interval it produces is the answer. In machine learning,
early stopping is a deliberate act, a regularizer chosen because validation loss
says further fitting would hurt. The conformal stop is not chosen against
anything. It is where the recipe happens to end, and as far as we can determine
no paper argues for it.

This paper treats the stop as an empirical question. The unit throughout is the
nat, the natural unit of the logarithmic score: a forecaster whose average log
density trails another's by $0.15$ nats assigns the realized outcomes
$e^{0.15}\approx 1.16$ times less probability per observation. Beside it we
report CRPS, the continuous ranked probability score, which integrates the
squared difference between the forecast's cumulative distribution and the
outcome's step function; it is in the units of the data, lower is better, and it
is the score most conformal papers report.

The paper has three components.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The count} (Section~\ref{sec:count}). In a frame of 686 arXiv
  papers, 599 stop at the empirical map. Papers that attack the pooled quantile
  replace it or reweight it; none keeps it and continues. The single flagged
  candidate for continuing, a PNAS paper, conditions before the map and stops
  after it.
\item \emph{The floor} (Section~\ref{sec:floor}). For a class of frozen
  conformal schemes, the excess log score over the conditional oracle
  decomposes exactly into a conditional mutual information fixed by the score
  and stratification, plus an estimation term. Calibration choices move only
  the estimation term. The identity says what stopping protects and what it
  abandons.
\item \emph{The price} (Section~\ref{sec:price}). Inside a transform grammar
  where the empirical map is one invertible step among peers, we hold one
  forecaster and one empirical object fixed and move only the object's
  position: last, as the convention has it, or interior, with modelling
  continuing on its output. On 17{,}364 economic series, stopping costs 0.40
  to 0.48 nats after a null-control adjustment; the interior placement
  recovers a third to a half of that; and the
  stopped arm's empirical coverage sits nearer the nominal target than the
  unconstrained base's, so coverage monitoring is blind to the entire loss.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:discussion} states the recommendation: keep the map, keep the
certificate where a certificate is wanted, and keep modelling past it.

\paragraph{What is new and what is borrowed.} The count and the price are this
paper's, and the floor identity is proved in Appendix~\ref{app:floor} with its
scope stated carefully. The published theory around the pooled shape is
context, not a contribution here: exact distribution-free conditional coverage
is impossible in nonatomic settings \citep{lei2014distribution,barber2021limits};
weighting calibration points unequally costs coverage in proportion to the
total-variation distance between their residual laws \citep{barber2023beyond};
and a guarantee held at a fixed point rather than on average inflates the
threshold for classical testing-power reasons
\citep{li1989honest,low1997nonparametric,cai2004adaptation}. Together these
give the pooled shape a defence. Nothing in them concerns the stop, which is
the point of this paper. The base forecaster is the subject of
\citet{cotton2026transforms}.

\section{The count}\label{sec:count}

The claim that everyone stops concerns published work, so it gets a protocol: a
defined frame, documented keyword rules, and full-text reading of every paper
whose label could change the conclusion. The frame is every arXiv paper with
``conformal prediction'' in the title at harvest date, $n=686$. Classifier,
frame, and per-paper verdicts are in the repository.

The object classified is the \emph{empirical map}: the step turning the multiset
of held-out errors into a distribution, concretely the empirical cumulative
distribution of the calibration scores. A paper \emph{stops} if the output of
that map, its quantiles, its $p$-values, or its intervals, is the answer. The
alternatives found in the frame: \emph{replace} the pooled quantile with a
conditional estimate (SPCI fits a quantile regression to recent residuals
\citep{xu2023spci}); \emph{tune} the level online while keeping the map
(adaptive conformal inference \citep{gibbs2021adaptive}); \emph{consume} the
output in a downstream decision such as screening pseudo-labels; or
\emph{compose}: keep the map and fit a model to its output, the move whose
absence is the finding.

Of 686 papers, 599 stop, 16 replace, 28 tune, 38 consume, and 5 propose no
method. One compose candidate was flagged by the screen: distributional
conformal prediction \citep{chernozhukov2021distributional}, which uses an
estimated conditional distribution as its score. Read in full, it conditions
\emph{before} the map, and the answer is the empirical quantile of the resulting
ranks: a richer score, then a stop. The refutation criterion for the count is
stated and falsifiable: a paper that retains the empirical rank transform,
applies it to residuals, and fits a downstream model on the transformed stream.
Reweighting the empirical law does not count, conditioning upstream does not
count, adapting the level does not count.

Outside the conformal banner the composing move is routine. The normal quantile
transform in hydrological forecasting \citep{bogner2012}, copula time-series
models \citep{chenfan2006}, and autoregressive transformation models
\citep{rugamer2023} all apply an empirical marginal map and keep modelling in
the transformed coordinates. The absence is a habit of one community, not a
difficulty of the idea.

A second classification, reported in the repository, checks how papers treat
residual pooling: 94.5\% never let the weights on calibration residuals depend
on the test point at any granularity, and each of the nine smooth-weighting
papers that claims exact validity was read in full and found to claim it
through a legitimate escape (marginal recalibration, a stated coverage gap, a
known covariate-shift tilt, or added smoothness assumptions). The field
polices the pooling constraint carefully. The stop it does not police at all.

\section{The floor}\label{sec:floor}

The identity below says what the calibration step can and cannot influence. It
needs two definitions and has a deliberately narrowed scope.

A \emph{nonconformity score} is a map $A:\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}\to
\mathbb{R}$ assigning a scalar to each candidate outcome $y$ at input $x$. A
\emph{stratification} $\kappa:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{K}$ assigns each input to
a category. Both are fit on a training fold and frozen, since exchangeability
of calibration and test scores is what the coverage argument uses. The schemes
covered by the proposition are those whose score is, for each $x$, a strictly
increasing absolutely continuous bijection in $y$: signed-residual and
normalized signed-residual schemes, and predictive systems built on invertible
score transformations. Folded scores such as the absolute residual
$|y-\widehat\mu(x)|$ and quantile-regression scores are not bijections in $y$
and need a separate treatment; the empirical arms in Section~\ref{sec:price}
are of the covered type.

Such a scheme emits the pooled empirical law of calibration scores in the test
point's stratum, pushed back through $A(x,\cdot)^{-1}$. For log scoring the
emitted law must have a density, so let $\widehat G_\kappa$ be an absolutely
continuous estimate of the pooled score law (a kernel-smoothed or interpolated
empirical law; the raw empirical measure is atomic and scores $-\infty$). Write
$W=A(X,Y)$, let $G_\kappa$ be the population pooled law, and let the benchmark
be the conditional oracle, whose expected negative log score is the conditional
entropy $H(Y\given X)$. Throughout, $L(\widehat F)=\E[-\log \widehat f_X(Y)]$
is the expected log loss of the conformal predictive, so an excess is positive
and smaller is better.

\begin{proposition}[The floor, exactly]\label{prop:floor}
Fix the training fold and the calibration sample, so $(A,\kappa,\widehat
G_\kappa)$ are deterministic, let $(X,Y)$ be a test pair independent of both,
and assume the laws below admit densities with finite divergences. Then the
excess log loss of the conformal predictor decomposes exactly as
\[
L(\widehat F) - H(Y\given X)
\;=\; \I(W;X\given\kappa)
\;+\; \E_{\kappa}\,\KLop\!\big(G_{\kappa}\,\|\,\widehat G_{\kappa}\big),
\]
where $\I(W;X\given\kappa)$ is the conditional mutual information between score
and input given the stratum. (Unconditionally, take the outer expectation over
the calibration sample; the first term is unchanged.) The second term is
nonnegative and is the only part any calibration choice, estimator, quantile
convention, randomization, or level, can affect. At a fixed calibration sample
it vanishes if and only if $\widehat G_\kappa=G_\kappa$ almost everywhere;
KL-consistent estimation of the pooled law with growing calibration mass per
stratum drives it to zero asymptotically, which is an assumption on the
estimator, not a consequence of the setup.
\end{proposition}

The proof is a change of variables in which the Jacobian of $A$ cancels between
truth and forecast, then a split at the population pooled law
(Appendix~\ref{app:floor}). In words: the input reaches the predictive only
through the frozen reshaping and the frozen stratum choice, so whatever
information about the score the input still carries within a stratum is lost
before calibration begins, and no amount of calibration data buys it back. With
a location score and one stratum the floor is $\I(R;X)$, the mutual information
between the model's error and its input.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:usecase}
The floor is zero exactly when the frozen pair renders the score independent of
the input within strata: $\I(W;X\given\kappa)=0$ iff $W\perp X\given\kappa$.
When $X$ carries the full information set available downstream, that is the
statement that the modelling was finished before the conformal step. Conformal
calibration is especially valuable when one is unwilling to assume that; but
its guarantee does not diagnose whether the floor is zero, and one may deploy
it on perfectly finished residuals purely for finite-sample validity. What the
identity rules out is the middle ground: whenever the floor is positive, no
calibration choice recovers any of it.
\end{remark}

The floor explains what stopping protects: the certificate, which concerns the
marginal rank of the test score and is genuinely exact at its position. It also
says what stopping abandons: $\I(W;X\given\kappa)$, the conditional information
the frozen score discarded. A downstream model consuming the map's output,
together with the input, is one means of recovering part of that quantity, not
an additional source of loss beyond it. The published results cited in
Section~\ref{sec:intro} defend the pooling constraint inside the certificate.
No result defends ending the pipeline. Whether ending it is expensive is a
measurement question, taken up next.

\section{The price}\label{sec:price}

\subsection{A laboratory where position is a variable}\label{sec:grammar}

Inside standard conformal practice the stop is invisible: the pipeline ends at
the map by construction, so there is nothing to vary. To measure the stop one
needs a setting where the conformal step is an ordinary component, and that
requires seeing it as what Section~\ref{sec:floor} showed it to be: a
univariate transform. The raw empirical map is a step function, not invertible
and without a density; repaired by strictly monotone interpolation through its
knots, with kernel-smoothed mass so it can be log-scored, it becomes an
invertible state-updated map of the observation coordinate, and such a map can
sit anywhere in a chain.

The laboratory is a transform grammar \citep{cotton2026transforms}. A
forecaster is a \emph{chain} of state-conditional invertible transforms, each
removing one kind of structure and passing its residual on: a location tracker
subtracts a running mean, a scale transform divides by a running deviation, a
difference removes trend. A \emph{terminal leaf} models the law of whatever
residual remains, and the predictive in the original coordinate is the leaf's
law pushed back through the inverse chain, change of variables handled exactly.
The base forecaster, \texttt{laplace}, runs roughly sixty candidate chains in
parallel, combines their predictive densities with likelihood weights updated
every observation, splices generalized Pareto tails onto the extremes, and
scores its own probability integral transforms online. It ranks first in the
public benchmark this study extends, and its own terminal leaf is tuned to
CRPS.

In this laboratory the conformal habit is one configuration among two:

\begin{description}
\item[stop.] Take the finished predictive, keep its centre, replace its shape
  with the pooled empirical law of the model's own recent errors,
  kernel-smoothed. Quote that. Nothing acts downstream. This arm is a frozen
  conformal scheme of the covered type, so Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}
  applies with floor $\I(R;X)$.
\item[compose-after.] Install the identical empirical object as the terminal
  leaf of \emph{every} candidate chain. Writing $R^c_t$ for chain $c$'s
  residual and $\widehat G^c_t$ for the pooled empirical law of that chain's
  own residual stream, the arm's predictive is
  \[
  \widehat F_t \;=\; \sum_c w^c_t \,\big(T^c_t\big)^{-1}_{\#}\widehat G^c_t,
  \]
  where $(T^c_t)^{-1}_{\#}$ pushes the map's law back through chain $c$'s
  inverse transform and the weights $w^c_t$ are likelihood weights fitted
  online to the realized densities the maps assign, that is, a mixture model
  fitted on the maps' output stream. Every predictive path passes through an
  empirical map: the leaf override replaces the terminal leaf of every
  candidate chain in the implementation, so the pool weights transform chains
  against each other, never the map against a parametric alternative, and no
  weight can route around it. The one exception is the generalized Pareto tail
  splice, which replaces the density beyond a frozen tail region; it acts on
  the extremes of the composed predictive and is shared with the base arm.
  This arm carries no coverage certificate. It is the move the floor identity
  permits, a change of representation, executed after the map rather than
  before it.
\end{description}

Both positions run flat and recency-weighted (exponential weights, half-life
150 observations), so staleness explains no comparison.

\subsection{Design}\label{sec:design}

The corpus is FRED economic change series (differences of rates, production,
employment and similar), non-price, with two tiers. The \emph{breadth} tier
uses 256 observations of history per forecast origin and every qualifying
series: 17{,}364. The \emph{deep} tier uses 1{,}024 observations and series with at
least 1{,}100, so the 400-residual window is a strict subset of the history it
could have used: about 6{,}400 series. Twelve
rolling origins per series, horizons 1, 6 and 12. Because origins within a
series overlap, all intervals are bootstrap over series, not rows.

A control corpus of synthetic independent draws (Gaussian, Student-$t_3$,
Laplace) runs under the identical protocol at matched context. On the control
there is no state to condition on, so what a constrained arm loses there is
finite-sample overhead: the cost of estimating a 41-node empirical law rather
than a parametric scale. That overhead is subtracted from every headline
number below, and we call the result \emph{null-control-adjusted} rather than
net of estimation, for two reasons the adjustment cannot fix. The estimation
term $\KLop(G\,\|\,\widehat G)$ depends on $G$, so its size on three simple
iid laws is a gauge, not an unbiased estimate of its size on economic
residuals; and because the base forecaster adapts, its residuals are not
exactly state-independent even on iid data. Raw deficits, without the
adjustment, are 0.51, 0.61 and 0.59 nats at horizons 1, 6 and 12 on the
breadth tier (paired, 208{,}368 rows per horizon): the adjustment removes
about a fifth of the raw gap, and both versions are reported so no reader
has to trust the gauge. A
sharper control, permuting each real series so the marginal residual law is
preserved while temporal state dependence is destroyed; where the two gauges
disagree, the permutation control governs.

\subsection{The decomposition, null-control-adjusted}\label{sec:decomp}

Breadth tier, 17{,}364 series, null-control-adjusted, log score deficit
against the unconstrained base:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
 & $h{=}1$ & $h{=}6$ & $h{=}12$ \\
\midrule
stop, total cost            & 0.397 & 0.475 & 0.462 \\
\quad of which pooled shape & 0.267 & 0.315 & 0.275 \\
\quad of which position     & 0.131 & 0.160 & 0.187 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Raw per-arm deficits carry series-bootstrap 95\% intervals of about $\pm 0.02$
throughout. Composing recovers 33\%, 34\% and 40\% of the cost at the three
horizons. The deep tier gives raw stop
costs of 0.34 to 0.40 nats with 40 to 55 percent recovered by the interior
placement.

The deep-tier fraction is not yet a pure position effect, and we flag the
confound rather than interpret past it. Between tiers, three things change at
once: 256 against 1{,}024 observations of context, the series panels differ,
and the 400-residual window truncates a 1{,}024-observation history while
holding all of a 256-observation one.
Nor is a 400-observation window inherent to stopping; a stopped map may retain
every residual it has ever seen. The clean separation is a same-series,
same-context ablation in which only one bit changes at a time: stop arms at
windows 250, 400, 750 and all history, and composed arms matched window for
window, on the ctx-1024 panel. Until that ablation
reports, the hypothesis that \emph{the price of stopping grows with how much
the pooled window discards} is exactly that, a hypothesis the current tiers
are consistent with but do not identify.

The arms are close on the median series; the mean cost is carried by a
minority of series where stopping hurts badly. Stopping behaves like the
removal of insurance.

Adaptivity does not substitute for position. Recency-weighting the stop arm's
window recovers 0.05 to 0.08 nats on the breadth tier, against 0.14 to 0.27
for moving the map's position, raw in both cases; and level adaptation is
dispatched by Proposition~\ref{prop:floor} directly, since it acts on the
pooled scores alone.

On CRPS the tiers disagree: on the deep tier the stopped arm degrades CRPS
and the composed arm improves it, while on the breadth tier both positions
sit within about 1\% of the base. The disagreement is reported, not resolved,
and CRPS appears in no summary of this paper.

\subsection{Empirical coverage is blind to the loss}\label{sec:blind}

Coverage is the axis conformal practice monitors, so we measure it directly,
with two scope notes. These are economic time series and we assert no
exchangeability, so what follows is empirical coverage, not the finite-sample
certificate of Section~\ref{sec:intro}; and the intervals are the 0.1 and 0.9
quantiles of the kernel-smoothed predictive, which need not coincide with the
raw conformal order-statistic endpoints. What the table tests is therefore the
monitoring practice: whether watching interval coverage would reveal the log
score loss. Breadth tier, paired rows, target 0.800:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
arm & coverage & distance from target \\
\midrule
unconstrained    & 0.870 & 0.070 \\
stop             & 0.784 & 0.016 \\
stop, weighted   & 0.799 & 0.001 \\
compose-after    & 0.797 & 0.003 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

We expected the stopped arm to show good overall coverage, and it does, along
with something the log score column would never suggest: every conformal arm
sits nearer nominal than the unconstrained base, which overcovers at 0.870.
On this axis the conformal arms win. On the log score axis the stopped arm
loses 0.40 to 0.48 nats. Both statements are true at once, which is the
point: coverage tracks the rank of the outcome in the predictive; the floor
tracks conditional information the frozen score discarded; the two move
independently, and a practice that monitors only coverage will never see the
loss.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The title is a description. The conformal recipe stops at its empirical map the
way training stops at an epoch: except that in learning the stop is chosen
against validation evidence, and here it is chosen against nothing. Measured,
the stop costs 0.40 to 0.48 nats after a null-control adjustment on more than
seventeen thousand series, and composing an ordinary model past the map
recovers a third to a half of that at no cost to empirical coverage. The floor identity
locates the part of the loss that is fixed before calibration begins, and
Remark~\ref{rem:usecase} states the corollary that matters in practice:
whenever the floor is positive, and the method is most attractive exactly when
one cannot rule that out, no calibration choice recovers any of it.

The recommendation is not a better calibrator, since every calibration choice
moves only the estimation term. It is to treat the empirical map as a
coordinate change with a certificate attached at its position, and to keep
modelling on its output. The composed arm is one constructive instance, and in
the sense of Remark~\ref{rem:usecase} it is conformal prediction with the
modelling finished; its scored calibration stream then measures, rather than
assumes, whether the modelling ever was.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The count is a keyword screen with full-text
verification of every label that could change the conclusion; the 599 stop
default rests on the screen plus a stated refutation criterion. The prices are
measured on one base forecaster and one economic corpus plus a synthetic
control; the deep tier and the matched-window ablation of
Section~\ref{sec:decomp} are incomplete. The null-control adjustment gauges finite-sample overhead on
simple iid laws and is not an unbiased estimate of the estimation term on
economic residuals. Scoring floors the log density at $-25$ per observation, which is not a
proper score where it binds, and it binds unequally: on 1.78\% of stopped-arm
rows against 0.62\% of base rows (breadth tier, paired). The inequality favours the
stopped arm, and tightening the floor stepwise from $-25$ to $-10$ shrinks
the raw stop deficit monotonically from 0.51 to 0.33 nats at horizon 1, so
every gap reported here is a lower bound on its unclipped value. The composed arm has no coverage
guarantee, which is the thing being priced, and it is not proposed as a
conformal method. The floor proposition covers invertible signed scores only;
folded and quantile scores need their own treatment.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}}\label{app:floor}

Fix the fold, so $(A,\kappa)$ is deterministic. Given $X=x$, change of
variables gives the true density $p(y\given x) = p_W(A(x,y)\given x)\,A'(x,y)$,
and the conformal density is $\widehat f_x(y) = \widehat
g_{\kappa(x)}(A(x,y))\,A'(x,y)$ with $\widehat g_\kappa$ the density of the
absolutely continuous estimate $\widehat G_\kappa$. The Jacobian cancels in the
log ratio, so
\[
\KLop\big(P(\cdot\given x)\,\|\,\widehat F_x\big)
= \KLop\big(P_W(\cdot\given x)\,\|\,\widehat G_{\kappa(x)}\big)
= \KLop\big(P_W(\cdot\given x)\,\|\,G_{\kappa(x)}\big)
+ \E_{W\sim P_W(\cdot\given x)}\log\frac{g_{\kappa(x)}(W)}{\widehat g_{\kappa(x)}(W)} .
\]
Averaging over $X$: the first term is
$\E_X\,\KLop(P_W(\cdot\given X)\,\|\,P_W(\cdot\given\kappa(X))) =
\I(W;X\given\kappa)$ by definition, and the second averages to
$\E_\kappa \KLop(G_\kappa\,\|\,\widehat G_\kappa) \ge 0$. Every calibration
operation is a function of the pooled scores alone, hence acts only on
$\widehat G_\kappa$ and the second term. \hfill$\square$
