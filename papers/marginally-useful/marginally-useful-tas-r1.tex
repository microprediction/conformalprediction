\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The conformal step adds no forecastability. A conformal predictive system pools one law for
its score across all inputs, and its expected log-score regret to the oracle is exactly
the mutual information between that score and the input, plus a marginal mismatch the
conformal step removes. The information the system can extract about the future is
therefore fixed by the model and score it wraps before calibration runs, and it can be
less than none: a poorly located model wrapped in conformal prediction can do worse than
ignoring the inputs altogether. On 572 economic time series, pooled raw residuals leave
at least 0.117 nats per observation to a feasible challenger, and a single scale transform
leaves none it can detect.
\end{abstract}

\section{The claim}

Conformal prediction is widely presented as the rigorous way to add uncertainty to a
forecast. One forecasting library tabulates methods by ``Calibration Guarantee'', awarding
the check mark to conformal prediction and a tilde to bootstrap, quantile regression and
ARIMA alike \citep{nixtla_conformal}. A popular introduction adds that with Bayesian
posteriors or bootstrapping ``we have no guarantees that these approaches will perform well
on new data'' \citep{molnar_conformal}, and from there it is a short step to conformal
prediction as the thing that ``converts any statistical, machine or deep learning model into
a probabilistic prediction model'' \citep{manokhin_neuralprophet}. The methodological
literature is more careful \citep{lei2014distribution,barber2021limits}, but it states its
cautions in the currency of coverage. The question a forecaster asks is in a different
currency: how much of what can be predicted does the method predict?

That question has a precise form. Under the logarithmic score the most any forecast can gain
over the unconditional distribution of $Y$ is the mutual information $I(Y;X)$ between the
outcome and the information set \citep{delsole2004}, and \citet{catt2026} makes this the
definition of \emph{forecastability}, resolved by horizon. We answer the question for
conformal prediction. A conformal predictive system captures $I(Y;X)-I(T;X)$ of the
available forecastability, where $T$ is the score it pools, and the conformal step
contributes none of it (Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} and Corollary~\ref{cor:forecastability}). The
share can be negative. Whatever a conformal system knows about the future, the model and
score it wraps knew before calibration ran.

None of this disputes the guarantee. Given exchangeable data, a fitted predictor and a
nonconformity score, split conformal prediction returns a set with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marginal}
  \Pr\!\big(Y_{n+1}\in C_\alpha(X_{n+1})\big)\;\ge\;1-\alpha,
\end{equation}
finite-sample and with no assumption on the data distribution
\citep{vovk2005algorithmic,lei2018distribution,angelopoulos2023gentle}. That is a statement
about coverage, averaged over $X_{n+1}$. Our result is about the log score, and it separates
cleanly from \eqref{eq:marginal}: the finite-sample guarantee is coverage, the log-score
statement is exact for every fixed pooled law and asymptotic only in identifying which law
the conformal system uses (Lemma~\ref{lem:ident}).

\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}

Let $(X,Y)$ have joint law $P$ on $\mathcal X\times\R$, with conditional density
$p(y\given x)$ and marginal density $p_Y$. In time series, $X$ is the past and $Y$ the value
$h$ steps ahead. The oracle forecasts with $p(\cdot\given x)$ and the unconditional
forecaster with $p_Y$. Following \citet{catt2026}, the forecastability of $Y$ given $X$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F}
  F \;=\; \E\log p(Y\given X)-\E\log p_Y(Y)\;=\;I(Y;X),
\end{equation}
the most any forecast can gain over the unconditional law in expected log score. For a
forecaster $q$, its \emph{exploitability} is $\E\log q(Y\given X)-\E\log p_Y(Y)$, which is
at most $F$ and can be negative.

\paragraph{Scores and pooling.} A score transform is a family of maps
$\phi_x:\R\to\mathcal Z$, one per input, each a strictly increasing $C^1$ bijection onto a
common open interval $\mathcal Z$, with $(x,y)\mapsto\phi_x(y)$ and
$(x,y)\mapsto\partial_y\phi_x(y)$ jointly measurable. Write $T=\phi_X(Y)$ for the score and
$P_T$, $p_T$ for its marginal law and density. The cases in use are
\begin{itemize}
  \item the signed residual $\phi_x(y)=y-\hat\mu(x)$, whose conformal predictive system (CPS)
  is that of \citet{vovk2019nonparametric};
  \item the normalized residual $\phi_x(y)=(y-\hat\mu(x))/\hat\sigma(x)$
  \citep{lei2018distribution};
  \item the estimated conditional rank $\phi_x(y)=\Phi^{-1}\big(\hat F(y\given x)\big)$,
  which is distributional conformal prediction \citep{chernozhukov2021distributional}.
\end{itemize}
The model ($\hat\mu$, $\hat\sigma$ or $\hat F$) is fitted on data disjoint from what follows
and is held fixed. A \emph{pooled forecaster} in the coordinates $\phi$ chooses one density
$h$ on $\mathcal Z$, the same for every input, and reports
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pooled}
  q_h(y\given x)\;=\;h\big(\phi_x(y)\big)\,\partial_y\phi_x(y).
\end{equation}
A conformal predictive system is a pooled forecaster whose $h$ is read off the calibration
scores. Conformal calibration sees the scores $T_i$ and never the inputs, so it can choose
$h$ and nothing else.

\section{The gap}\label{sec:gap}

\begin{theorem}[Information gap of a pooled forecaster]\label{thm:gap}
Assume $T$ has finite differential entropy, $I(T;X)<\infty$, and $\E|\log h(T)|<\infty$.
Then for every density $h$ on $\mathcal Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
  \E\log p(Y\given X)-\E\log q_h(Y\given X)\;=\;I(T;X)+\KL(P_T\,\|\,H).
\end{equation}
The regret is minimized at $h=p_T$, where it equals $I(T;X)$. No choice of $h$, and no
recalibration that ignores $X$, brings it below $I(T;X)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the change of variables $t=\phi_x(y)$, $p(y\given x)=p_T(\phi_x(y)\given x)\,
\partial_y\phi_x(y)$, where $p_T(\cdot\given x)$ is the conditional density of $T$. The
Jacobians cancel in the ratio with \eqref{eq:pooled}, so
\[
\E\log\frac{p(Y\given X)}{q_h(Y\given X)}
=\E\log\frac{p_T(T\given X)}{h(T)}
=\E\log\frac{p_T(T\given X)}{p_T(T)}+\E\log\frac{p_T(T)}{h(T)}
=I(T;X)+\KL(P_T\,\|\,H),
\]
each term finite under the assumptions. The second term is non-negative and vanishes only
at $h=p_T$ (Gibbs). An $X$-blind recalibration applies a fixed increasing map $\psi$ to the
score, and $I(\psi(T);X)=I(T;X)$ because $\psi$ is invertible, so the floor is unchanged.
\end{proof}

The identity \eqref{eq:gap} is the chain rule for relative entropy along a product
reference, the split of redundancy into a dependence term and a marginal-mismatch term
familiar from universal coding \citep{clarke1990,grunwald2007}. We claim the reading, not
the algebra: it holds exactly, at every sample size, for the whole class of pooled
forecasters, which is the class every conformal predictive system belongs to. For the
signed residual it gives the residual gap $I(R;X)$ with $R=Y-\hat\mu(X)$. The form in
general coordinates is also stated by \citet{cotton2026gap}, who use it to measure the gap
along a sequence of transforms. Reading the symmetric
intervals of ordinary absolute-residual conformal as a distribution pools the symmetrized
law $h_{\mathrm{sym}}(z)=\tfrac12(p_R(z)+p_R(-z))$, and \eqref{eq:gap} then adds
$\KL(P_R\,\|\,H_{\mathrm{sym}})\le\log 2$ for discarding the sign.

\begin{lemma}[Which law the conformal system pools]\label{lem:ident}
Let the calibration pairs $(X_i,Y_i)$, $i=1,\dots,n$, be i.i.d.\ copies of $(X,Y)$, or a
stationary ergodic sequence with that marginal law, and let $\widehat G_n$ be the empirical
distribution function of the scores $T_i=\phi_{X_i}(Y_i)$. The signed CPS reports the
predictive distribution function $\Pi_x(y)=\widehat G_n(\phi_x(y))$, up to the $1/(n+1)$
fence-post convention, and $\sup_t|\widehat G_n(t)-G_T(t)|\to0$ almost surely, by
Glivenko--Cantelli in the i.i.d.\ case and by the ergodic theorem applied on a countable
dense set of $t$ in the stationary case. If a smoothed version with density $\hat h_n$
satisfies $\KL(P_T\,\|\,\hat H_n)\to0$, the regret \eqref{eq:gap} of the smoothed CPS tends
to $I(T;X)$.
\end{lemma}

Exchangeability alone does not give this. An infinite exchangeable sequence is a mixture of
i.i.d.\ sequences, and $\widehat G_n$ then converges to the distribution function of the
directing measure, which is random. Coverage \eqref{eq:marginal} needs only
exchangeability; the identification needs more. A finite empirical CPS is a predictive
distribution but not a Lebesgue density, which is why the log-score statement is made for a
smoothed version. Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} needs none of this, because it is a statement about
every fixed $h$.

\begin{corollary}[What a conformal system captures]\label{cor:forecastability}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}, and with $I(Y;X)<\infty$, the pooled
forecaster \eqref{eq:pooled} has exploitability
\begin{equation}\label{eq:captured}
  \E\log q_h(Y\given X)-\E\log p_Y(Y)\;=\;I(Y;X)-I(T;X)-\KL(P_T\,\|\,H).
\end{equation}
Its best value, $I(Y;X)-I(T;X)$, is fixed by the score before calibration. The fraction of
forecastability captured is at most $1-I(T;X)/I(Y;X)$, which is negative whenever
$I(T;X)>I(Y;X)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Subtract \eqref{eq:gap} from \eqref{eq:F}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the calibration gain is not forecastability]\label{rem:calgain}
The conformal step moves $h$ toward $p_T$ and so raises the score by removing
$\KL(P_T\,\|\,H)$. That term is a property of the marginal law of $T$ alone. It is the same
whether $X$ carries a great deal of information about $Y$ or none, and a forecaster with
$I(Y;X)=0$ gains it just as much. What depends on $X$ is $I(Y;X)-I(T;X)$, and that is set by
$\phi$. In this sense the conformal step adds no forecastability: it repairs marginal
miscalibration, and what the system knows about the future is decided upstream.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Negative capture]
The corollary's negative case is not exotic. Let $Y$ be independent of $X$, so there is
nothing to forecast, and let $\hat\mu$ vary with $x$. Then $R=Y-\hat\mu(X)$ depends on $X$
through the model alone, $I(R;X)>0=I(Y;X)$, and the conformal predictive system, perfectly
calibrated in coverage, scores below the unconditional law by $I(R;X)$. The coverage
certificate is silent about this.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Cells and conditional methods]\label{rem:cells}
Mondrian or binned conformal predictive systems \citep{bostrom2021mondrian,toccaceli2026crps}
pool one law per cell $g(X)$. The same computation with $H$ replaced by a family $H_g$ gives
the floor $I(T;X\given g(X))=I(T;X)-I(T;g(X))$: calibration per cell captures
$I(T;g(X))$ and no more. Distributional conformal prediction
\citep{chernozhukov2021distributional} is Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} with
$\phi_x=\Phi^{-1}\circ\hat F(\cdot\given x)$, so its floor is $I(T;X)$ for the estimated
rank, which is zero when $\hat F$ is the true conditional law. That is the sense in which it
answers the problem posed here, and it does so through $\hat F$. Density-based and
conditional-histogram constructions
\citep{izbicki2020,izbicki2022,sesia2021}, localized conformal prediction \citep{guan2023}
and conformalized quantile regression \citep{romano2019conformalized} all put $X$ into the
score or the reference class. Each reduces the floor by what its conditioning retains, never
by the calibration step.
\end{remark}

\section{Two readings}\label{sec:readings}

\paragraph{A false-pooling cost.} A pooled forecaster asserts that once $\phi$ is applied,
one law fits every input, which is to say $T\perp X$. The gap $I(T;X)$ is the relative
entropy from the true joint law to the nearest such independence model, and one pays it
whether or not one notices making the assumption.

\paragraph{A betting rent.} A gambler who knows a distribution $P$ and bets against a fair
book priced by $Q$ compounds wealth at expected log-rate $\KL(P\,\|\,Q)$ per round
\citep{kelly1956new,cover2006elements}. Read the pooled law as the book and the conditional
law as what the oracle knows; the oracle's expected rent per round is $I(T;X)$, and a
re-levelling that ignores $X$ leaves it unchanged.

\paragraph{Coverage and log score grade different objects.} Two forecasters with the same
location $\hat\mu$ but different claimed densities $f$ have identical residual conformal
sets and identical coverage at every level, while their log scores differ without bound:
take $X$ degenerate, $Y\sim N(0,1)$ and $f_\sigma=N(0,\sigma^2)$, whose expected log score
$-\tfrac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)-1/(2\sigma^2)$ diverges at both ends of the $\sigma$ range
while the set never moves. This does not conflict with Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}, where the
degenerate $X$ gives $I(T;X)=0$. The example grades a density $f$ that the conformal set
never consults; the theorem grades the density the conformal system itself reports. If the
practitioner replaces a misspecified $f$ by the conformal system's output, the score can
rise, by the $\KL$ term of Remark~\ref{rem:calgain}, and never past the ceiling
$I(Y;X)-I(T;X)$.

\section{Evidence}\label{sec:evidence}

\paragraph{Exact checks.} The identities are checked by deterministic grid integration, each
side computed independently from its definition (\texttt{check\_gap.py}). With $X$ uniform on
$[0,1]$ and $Y\given x$ skew-normal with mean $2\sin 3x$ and scale $0.5+1.5x$:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
location model $\hat\mu(x)$ & $I(Y;X)$ & $I(R;X)$ & captured & share\\
\midrule
correct, $2\sin 3x$ & $0.340$ & $0.118$ & $0.222$ & $0.65$\\
shrunk, $1.2\sin 3x$ & $0.340$ & $0.152$ & $0.189$ & $0.56$\\
$Y\perp X$, $\hat\mu(x)=1.5\cos 4x$ & $0$ & $0.653$ & $-0.653$ & n/a\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Both sides of \eqref{eq:gap} and \eqref{eq:captured} agree to within $10^{-8}$ nats. With the
same truth and a misspecified logistic $\hat F$, the distributional-conformal score has
$I(T;X)=0.070$ against $I(R;X)=0.123$ for raw residuals under the same location: the score
transform, not the calibration, halves the gap.

\paragraph{Economic time series.} \citet{cotton2026gap} measures the residual gap on 572
continuous FRED change series, with a strong online forecaster's mean held fixed and the
past as $X$. The oracle is unavailable, but the gap is still observable from below. If $M$
is the pooled law estimated from the past and $Q$ any past-measurable challenger, then
$\E\log(q/m)=\E\,\KL(P\,\|\,M)-\E\,\KL(P\,\|\,Q)\le\E\,\KL(P\,\|\,M)$, so a challenger's
held-out gain over the pooled law is, in expectation, a lower bound on the regret the pooled
system pays to the oracle. The held-out gains, in nats per observation, were:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
pooled stream & challenger gain & $90\%$ interval & positive on\\
\midrule
raw residuals & $+0.117$ & $[0.105,\,0.129]$ & $84\%$ of series\\
standardized by an EWMA scale & $-0.020$ & $[-0.060,\,0.015]$ & $42\%$\\
serially whitened & $-0.016$ & $[-0.058,\,0.017]$ & $40\%$\\
PIT-normalized & $+0.003$ & $[-0.006,\,0.012]$ & $26\%$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Pooling raw residuals, which is what split conformal does, leaves at least $0.117$ nats per
observation on the table. One scale transform removes all of it that this challenger can
find. The calibration step is the same in every row; the rows differ only in the
coordinates in which the pooling happens. In a companion simulation with a known
heteroscedastic truth, raw and normalized split conformal both hit $90\%$ marginal coverage,
while raw pooling covers $99.8\%$ of the calmest volatility quartile and $76.7\%$ of the
most volatile \citep{cotton2026gap}.

\section{What this does not say}\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{The guarantee stands.} Conformal prediction certifies the coverage of a set,
finite-sample and distribution-free, under exchangeability. Where coverage is the
deliverable, as in selective prediction, shortlisting, or conformal $p$-values for novelty
detection \citep{angelopoulos2021uncertainty,bates2021distribution}, it is the right tool,
and within its pooled class the CPS is log-score optimal. The cost is the class.

\paragraph{Terms.} Marginal coverage is one calibration-type property of a set. It is not
probabilistic calibration of a predictive distribution, and neither is sharpness
\citep{gneiting2007probabilistic,gneiting2007strictly}. We use \emph{coverage} for the first,
\emph{calibration} only for the distributional property, and grade forecasts by the log
score.

\paragraph{Heteroscedasticity, not covariate density.} The gap is driven by how the law of
$T$ varies with $x$. It has nothing to do with whether $x$ is typical or unusual under
$p_X$: a rare input with ordinary noise contributes nothing to $I(T;X)$, and marginal
coverage alone does not say which inputs are over- or under-covered.

\paragraph{The log score.} The exact information identities belong to the log score. For
CRPS or the interval score the analogous floor is the regret of the best $X$-blind law under
that score, which is not an information quantity, and we claim no identity for it.

\paragraph{Estimate first, certify second.} A Bayesian posterior predictive, or any rich
conditional model, can supply the distribution, with conformal calibration added on top as a
certificate. The theorem says what that order buys: the certificate costs nothing in
forecastability if the model has already made $T$ nearly independent of $X$, and cannot
recover what the model left in $I(T;X)$. Conformalize last, and put the conditioning in the
score.

\paragraph{Related work.} The pieces are known: the CPS \citep{vovk2019nonparametric}, the
calibration--sharpness paradigm \citep{gneiting2007probabilistic}, the impossibility of
distribution-free conditional coverage \citep{lei2014distribution,barber2021limits}, an
information-theoretic reading of set size \citep{correia2024information}, the observation
that validity invites weak predictors \citep{recht2024cover} and that set length can be
gamed at fixed coverage \citep{min2026coveragelength}, and forecastability as mutual
information \citep{delsole2004,catt2026}. To our knowledge the exact regret of the pooled
class, its identification with the conformal predictive system, and the resulting account
of how much forecastability conformal prediction captures have not been stated before.

\section{Conclusion}

Coverage answers the question \emph{is the truth in this region?} In forecasting that is
often not the question anyone was asking. The question is how much of what can be predicted
has been predicted, and for conformal prediction the answer is exact: $I(Y;X)-I(T;X)$ at
best, set by the model and the score, with the conformal step contributing nothing that
depends on the inputs. The price of the split conformal coverage guarantee, paid in the
forecaster's own currency, is $I(T;X)$.

\texttt{check\_gap.py}, supplied with the submission\ifanon\else{} and at the repository

list are public.
