\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The conformal step adds no forecastability. A conformal predictive system pools one law for
its score across all inputs, and its log-score regret to the oracle is exactly the mutual
information between score and input, plus a marginal mismatch that calibration removes.
What the system knows about the future is therefore fixed before calibration runs, and it
can be less than nothing. Pooling is a decision to stop transforming, and the same identity
prices every stopping point. The conformal literature almost always stops at the pooled
rank map, and on 572 economic time series that stop leaves a measurable gap which one
scale transform removes.
\end{abstract}

\section{The claim}

Conformal prediction is sold to forecasters as the rigorous way to add uncertainty, the thing
that ``converts any statistical, machine or deep learning model into a probabilistic
prediction model'' \citep{manokhin_neuralprophet}; one forecasting library awards it alone
the check mark for ``Calibration Guarantee'' \citep{nixtla_conformal}. The guarantee is
real: under exchangeability split conformal prediction returns a set with
$\Pr(Y_{n+1}\in C_\alpha(X_{n+1}))\ge1-\alpha$, finite-sample and distribution-free
\citep{vovk2005algorithmic,lei2018distribution}. But it is a statement about coverage,
averaged over inputs, and a forecaster's question is different: how much of what can be
predicted does the method predict?

Under the log score that question has an exact form. The most any forecast can gain over the
unconditional law of $Y$ is the mutual information $I(Y;X)$ \citep{delsole2004}, which
\citet{catt2026} makes the definition of \emph{forecastability}. We show that a conformal
predictive system captures at most $I(Y;X)-I(T;X)$ of it, where $T$ is the score it pools,
and that the conformal step contributes none of it (Section~\ref{sec:gap}). Pooling is a
choice of where to stop transforming the data, the same identity prices every stopping
point, and a coded survey of the literature shows the field stops at one point almost
without exception (Section~\ref{sec:stop}). On 572 economic series that stop costs a
measurable amount, and one scale transform recovers it (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}).

\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}

Let $(X,Y)$ have law $P$, with conditional density $p(y\given x)$ and marginal density
$p_Y$; in time series $X$ is the past and $Y$ the value $h$ steps ahead. The forecastability
of $Y$ given $X$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F}
  F \;=\; \E\log p(Y\given X)-\E\log p_Y(Y)\;=\;I(Y;X),
\end{equation}
and the \emph{exploitability} of a forecaster $q$ is $\E\log q(Y\given X)-\E\log p_Y(Y)$,
which is at most $F$ and can be negative \citep{catt2026}.

A score transform is a family of strictly increasing $C^1$ bijections $\phi_x:\R\to\mathcal
Z$ onto a common open interval, jointly measurable in $(x,y)$ with their derivatives. Write
$T=\phi_X(Y)$, with marginal law $P_T$ and density $p_T$. The signed residual
$\phi_x(y)=y-\hat\mu(x)$ gives the conformal predictive system (CPS) of
\citet{vovk2019nonparametric}; the normalized residual $(y-\hat\mu(x))/\hat\sigma(x)$ gives
normalized conformal prediction \citep{lei2018distribution}; the estimated conditional rank
$\Phi^{-1}(\hat F(y\given x))$ gives distributional conformal prediction
\citep{chernozhukov2021distributional}. The model is fitted on separate data and held
fixed. A \emph{pooled forecaster} chooses one density $h$ on $\mathcal Z$ for every input and
reports
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pooled}
  q_h(y\given x)\;=\;h\big(\phi_x(y)\big)\,\partial_y\phi_x(y).
\end{equation}
A CPS is a pooled forecaster whose $h$ is read off the calibration scores. Calibration sees
the scores and never the inputs, so it chooses $h$ and nothing else.

\section{The gap}\label{sec:gap}

\begin{theorem}[Information gap of a pooled forecaster]\label{thm:gap}
Assume $T$ has finite differential entropy, $I(T;X)<\infty$, and $\E|\log h(T)|<\infty$.
Then for every density $h$ on $\mathcal Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
  \E\log p(Y\given X)-\E\log q_h(Y\given X)\;=\;I(T;X)+\KL(P_T\,\|\,H).
\end{equation}
The regret is minimized at $h=p_T$, where it equals $I(T;X)$. No choice of $h$, and no
recalibration that ignores $X$, brings it below $I(T;X)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With $t=\phi_x(y)$, $p(y\given x)=p_T(\phi_x(y)\given x)\,\partial_y\phi_x(y)$, so the
Jacobians cancel against \eqref{eq:pooled} and
\[
\E\log\frac{p(Y\given X)}{q_h(Y\given X)}
=\E\log\frac{p_T(T\given X)}{p_T(T)}+\E\log\frac{p_T(T)}{h(T)}
=I(T;X)+\KL(P_T\,\|\,H),
\]
each term finite under the assumptions. The second term vanishes only at $h=p_T$ (Gibbs).
An $X$-blind recalibration applies a fixed increasing map $\psi$ to the score, and
$I(\psi(T);X)=I(T;X)$.
\end{proof}

The identity is the chain rule for relative entropy along a product reference, familiar
from universal coding as the split of redundancy into dependence and marginal mismatch
\citep{clarke1990,grunwald2007}. What is new is the reading: it holds exactly, at every
sample size, for the whole class every conformal predictive system belongs to. Reading the
symmetric intervals of absolute-residual conformal as a distribution adds
$\KL(P_R\,\|\,H_{\mathrm{sym}})\le\log 2$ for discarding the sign.

\begin{lemma}[Which law the conformal system pools]\label{lem:ident}
Let the calibration pairs be i.i.d.\ copies of $(X,Y)$, or stationary ergodic with that
marginal law, and let $\widehat G_n$ be the empirical distribution function of
$T_i=\phi_{X_i}(Y_i)$. The signed CPS reports $\Pi_x(y)=\widehat G_n(\phi_x(y))$ up to the
$1/(n+1)$ fence-post convention, and $\sup_t|\widehat G_n(t)-G_T(t)|\to0$ almost surely
(Glivenko--Cantelli, or the ergodic theorem on a countable dense set of $t$). If a smoothed
version with density $\hat h_n$ has $\KL(P_T\,\|\,\hat H_n)\to0$, its regret tends to
$I(T;X)$.
\end{lemma}

Exchangeability alone does not give this: an infinite exchangeable sequence is a mixture of
i.i.d.\ sequences, and $\widehat G_n$ converges to a random limit. Coverage needs only
exchangeability; the identification needs more, and the log score applies to a smoothed CPS
because a finite empirical CPS has no Lebesgue density. Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} needs none of
this, since it holds for every fixed $h$.

\begin{corollary}[What a conformal system captures]\label{cor:forecastability}
If also $I(Y;X)<\infty$, the pooled forecaster has exploitability
\begin{equation}\label{eq:captured}
  \E\log q_h(Y\given X)-\E\log p_Y(Y)\;=\;I(Y;X)-I(T;X)-\KL(P_T\,\|\,H),
\end{equation}
at most $I(Y;X)-I(T;X)$, a quantity fixed by the score before calibration. It is negative
whenever $I(T;X)>I(Y;X)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Subtract \eqref{eq:gap} from \eqref{eq:F}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the calibration gain is not forecastability]\label{rem:calgain}
Calibration moves $h$ toward $p_T$ and removes $\KL(P_T\,\|\,H)$. That term depends on the
marginal law of $T$ alone: a forecaster whose inputs carry no information gains it just as
much. What depends on $X$ is $I(Y;X)-I(T;X)$, and $\phi$ sets it. This is the sense in which
the conformal step adds no forecastability. The negative case is ordinary: if $Y\perp X$
but $\hat\mu$ varies with $x$, the residual depends on $X$ through the model alone, and a
CPS with perfect coverage scores below the unconditional law by $I(R;X)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Cells and conditional methods]\label{rem:cells}
A Mondrian CPS \citep{bostrom2021mondrian,toccaceli2026crps} pools per cell $g(X)$ and has
floor $I(T;X\given g(X))$, so per-cell calibration captures $I(T;g(X))$ and no more.
Distributional conformal prediction \citep{chernozhukov2021distributional} has floor
$I(T;X)$ for its estimated rank, zero when $\hat F$ is the true conditional law: it answers
the problem through $\hat F$. Density-based, histogram, localized and quantile constructions
\citep{izbicki2020,izbicki2022,sesia2021,guan2023,romano2019conformalized} likewise put $X$
into the score or the reference class, and each lowers the floor by what that conditioning
retains, never by the calibration step. Coverage and log score also grade different objects:
two forecasters with the same $\hat\mu$ and different claimed densities have identical
residual conformal sets while their log scores differ without bound, because the set never
consults the density; Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} grades the density the CPS itself reports.
\end{remark}

\section{Pooling is a stopping decision}\label{sec:stop}

A forecaster can be written as a chain of transformations of the data: remove a location,
divide by a scale, whiten serial dependence, map to ranks, and so on, each a
state-dependent bijection. At some point the chain stops and one law is pooled for what is
left. Split conformal prediction stops after the location, at the rank map of the
residuals. Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} prices any stop: stopping at coordinates $\phi$ costs
exactly $I(\phi_X(Y);X)$, and stopping at the true conditional rank costs nothing.

Two cautions keep this honest. The price need not fall as the chain grows, since an
$x$-dependent map can raise $I(T;X)$ as well as lower it; the theorem prices each stop and
does not say deeper is better. And the theorem is a population statement. With finite data
an early stop regularizes, as early stopping does in estimation, so a stop can win series by
series while losing on average.

\paragraph{Where the literature stops.} We classified every paper on arXiv with
``conformal prediction'' in its title, \prov{686} papers to \prov{August 2026}, by what it
does to the pooled rank map (protocol in Appendix~\ref{app:survey}). A method can
\emph{stop} there and quote the pooled law; \emph{replace} the pooled law with a conditional
estimate; \emph{tune} its level online; \emph{consume} the conformal output in a downstream
decision; or \emph{compose}, keeping the map and fitting a model on its output.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrl}
\toprule
move & papers & examples\\
\midrule
stop & \prov{599} & split conformal, CPS, CQR, Mondrian and normalized scores\\
replace & \prov{16} & SPCI, localized conformal, HopCPT, EnbPI\\
tune & \prov{28} & adaptive conformal inference, conformal PID\\
consume & \prov{38} & conformal risk control, selection\\
compose & \prov{0} & none found\\
no method & \prov{5} & surveys, applications\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The pattern is not an oversight of forecasting as a whole: mapping to empirical ranks and
then continuing to model is standard in meta-Gaussian forecasting and copula time series
\citep{bogner2012normal,chenfan2006}. It is a habit of the conformal literature, which knows
the pooled law is the weak point and always swaps it out rather than keeping it and
continuing.

\section{Evidence}\label{sec:evidence}

\paragraph{Exact checks.} Both sides of \eqref{eq:gap} and \eqref{eq:captured} are computed
independently by grid integration (\texttt{check\_gap.py}) and agree to within $10^{-8}$
nats. With $X$ uniform on $[0,1]$ and $Y\given x$ skew-normal with mean $2\sin3x$ and scale
$0.5+1.5x$, $I(Y;X)=0.340$. A correct location model leaves $I(R;X)=0.118$ and so captures
$65\%$ of it; shrinking the location to $1.2\sin3x$ captures $56\%$. When $Y\perp X$ and
$\hat\mu(x)=1.5\cos4x$, the CPS captures $-0.653$ nats. A misspecified logistic $\hat F$
used as a distributional-conformal score gives $I(T;X)=0.070$ against $0.123$ for raw
residuals under the same location: the score, not the calibration, halves the gap.

\paragraph{Coverage is blind to where the gap falls.} With $X$ uniform, $Y=\sigma(X)
\varepsilon$, $\sigma(x)=0.5+2x$, $\varepsilon\sim N(0,1)$, raw and normalized split
conformal share calibration sets of 500 and the rank index at $\alpha=0.1$ (400
replications of 20,000 test points; standard errors at most $0.001$):
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccccc}
\toprule
 & marginal & $\sigma$-quartile 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & width & interval score\\
\midrule
raw & $0.901$ & $0.998$ & $0.964$ & $0.873$ & $0.767$ & $5.37$ & $7.26$\\
normalized & $0.901$ & $0.900$ & $0.901$ & $0.901$ & $0.900$ & $4.96$ & $6.21$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The certificate is identical in both rows. The difference is entirely the coordinates in
which the pooling happens.

\paragraph{572 economic series.} We use the continuous FRED change series (572 of 701
enumerated, the rest too short)\footnote{This table, the simulation above and the
continuation result below first appeared in a working paper \citep{cotton2026gap}, which this
article supersedes.} and a strong online forecaster, \emph{laplace}
\citep{cotton_skaters}, whose predictive mean is held fixed with the past as $X$. The oracle
is unavailable, but the gap is observable from below: if $M$ is the pooled law estimated from
the past and $Q$ any past-measurable challenger, then
$\E\log(q/m)=\E\,\KL(P\,\|\,M)-\E\,\KL(P\,\|\,Q)\le\E\,\KL(P\,\|\,M)$, so a challenger's
held-out gain over the pooled law is, in expectation, a lower bound on what the pooled system
pays the oracle. Pooling at four successive stops of the transform chain, with laplace itself
run on the stream as challenger (nats per observation, series-bootstrap $90\%$ intervals):
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
stop at & challenger gain & $90\%$ interval & positive on\\
\midrule
raw residual (split conformal) & $+0.117$ & $[0.105,\,0.129]$ & $84\%$ of series\\
standardized by an EWMA scale & $-0.020$ & $[-0.060,\,0.015]$ & $42\%$\\
serially whitened & $-0.016$ & $[-0.058,\,0.017]$ & $40\%$\\
PIT-normalized & $+0.003$ & $[-0.006,\,0.012]$ & $26\%$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Stopping where split conformal stops leaves at least $0.117$ nats per observation. One scale
transform leaves nothing this challenger can find. The calibration step is the same in every
row.

\paragraph{Continuing past the rank map.} The composing move that the survey finds unused
does work. On the same series, keeping the rank map and fitting an AR(1) on its output beats
stopping at it by $0.038$ nats per observation on average (interval $0.027$ to $0.052$), and
lowers the mean per-series CRPS ratio by $3.2\%$. The gain is skewed: the median series is a
coin flip, and on i.i.d.\ null series the comparison reverses by $0.011$ to $0.051$ nats, the
estimation cost of refitting when there is nothing to find. Stopping is defensible series by
series and beaten on the corpus, as Section~\ref{sec:stop} anticipates.

\section{What this does not say}\label{sec:scope}

The coverage guarantee stands, and where coverage is the deliverable (selective prediction,
shortlisting, conformal $p$-values) conformal prediction is the right tool
\citep{angelopoulos2021uncertainty,bates2021distribution}. Within the pooled class the CPS is
log-score optimal; the cost is the class. We use \emph{coverage} for the property of a set
and \emph{calibration} only for the distributional property \citep{gneiting2007probabilistic}.
The gap is driven by how the law of $T$ varies with $x$, not by whether $x$ is typical under
$p_X$. The information identities belong to the log score; for CRPS or the interval score the
analogous floor is not an information quantity and we claim no identity for it. Finally, the
order matters: a Bayesian predictive or other conditional model can supply the distribution
and conformal calibration can certify it, and then the certificate costs no forecastability
if the model has made $T$ nearly independent of $X$. Conformalize last, and put the
conditioning in the score.

\paragraph{Related work.} The CPS \citep{vovk2019nonparametric}, the calibration--sharpness
paradigm \citep{gneiting2007probabilistic}, the impossibility of distribution-free conditional
coverage \citep{lei2014distribution,barber2021limits}, information-theoretic readings of set
size \citep{correia2024information}, the observations that validity invites weak predictors
\citep{recht2024cover} and that length can be gamed at fixed coverage
\citep{min2026coveragelength}, and forecastability as mutual information
\citep{delsole2004,catt2026} are all prior. To our knowledge the exact regret of the pooled
class, its identification with the conformal predictive system, the resulting account of the
forecastability conformal prediction captures, and the pricing of the stop the literature
makes have not been stated before.

\section{Conclusion}

Coverage answers \emph{is the truth in this region?} A forecaster asks how much of what can be
predicted has been predicted. For conformal prediction the answer is $I(Y;X)-I(T;X)$ at best,
set by the model and the score, with the calibration step contributing nothing that depends on
the inputs. The price of the coverage guarantee, paid in the forecaster's own currency, is
$I(T;X)$, and it is the price of stopping where the field stops.
