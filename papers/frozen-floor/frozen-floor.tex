\title{A Mechanical Characterization of Conformal Prediction\\ and its Irreducible Excess Risk}
\author{Peter Cotton\\\texttt{peter.cotton@microprediction.com}}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Every split, full, normalized, Mondrian, quantile, and predictive-system conformal
scheme produces the same shape of object: a frozen monotone reshaping of a
label-blind, stratum-pooled empirical law. We make that statement mechanical, so it
can be checked on any scheme by naming two ingredients, a score map and a
stratification. We then show that the excess logarithmic risk of any such scheme over
the conditional oracle equals, in the large-calibration limit, a single conditional
mutual information between the nonconformity score and the input given the stratum.
The quantity depends only on the two frozen ingredients, so no amount of calibration
reduces it. It is positive unless the frozen ingredients already render the score
conditionally independent of the input, that is, unless the modeling was finished
before the conformal step. The method's stated use case, doubt that the residual law
is conditionally adequate, is exactly the condition under which its floor is
positive. A corollary recovers the regressogram rate handicap of universally
consistent conformal predictive systems from the same constraint.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}

Let $(X,Y)$ have joint law $P$ on $\cX \times \R$, with conditional law $P(\cdot\given X)$
and conditional density $p(\cdot\given x)$. The benchmark is the conditional oracle
$P(\cdot\given X)$, whose expected logarithmic loss is the conditional entropy
$H(Y\given X)$. For a predictive law $F = \{F_x\}$ with density $f_x$ we score by the
logarithmic rule and measure a forecaster by its \emph{excess risk}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:excess}
  L(F) - H(Y\given X)
  \;=\; \E_X\!\left[\, \KL\big(P(\cdot\given X)\,\big\|\,F_X\big) \,\right] \;\ge\; 0 ,
\end{equation}
the average Kullback--Leibler divergence from the truth to the forecast. Excess risk is
zero if and only if the forecaster is the oracle almost surely.

A conformal scheme is fixed by two objects and a fold. A \emph{nonconformity score}
$A : \cX \times \R \to \R$ assigns a scalar $A(x,y)$ to a candidate value $y$ at $x$. A
\emph{stratification} $\kappa : \cX \to \cK$ assigns each $x$ to a category. Both are
computed from a training fold that is disjoint from the calibration data. The
calibration multiset $D_n = \{Z_i = (X_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ enters only through the pooled
scores. Write $\widehat G_\kappa$ for the empirical distribution of the calibration
scores in stratum $\kappa$,
\[
  \widehat G_\kappa
  \;=\; \text{empirical law of } \{\, A(Z_i) : \kappa(X_i) = \kappa \,\}.
\]

\section{The invariant}

\begin{definition}[Conformal predictor]\label{def:conf}
The conformal predictive distribution function at $x$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cdf}
  \widehat F_x(y) \;=\; \widehat G_{\kappa(x)}\!\big( A(x,y) \big) .
\end{equation}
Its prediction set at level $\alpha$ is the sublevel set
$\{\, y : A(x,y) \le \widehat Q_{1-\alpha}(\kappa(x)) \,\}$, where
$\widehat Q_{1-\alpha}(\kappa)$ is the $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of $\widehat G_\kappa$.
\end{definition}

Definition~\ref{def:conf} is the split, Mondrian, normalized, quantile, and
predictive-system output written once; full conformal is the case in which $A$ depends
symmetrically on the augmented multiset, treated in Section~\ref{sec:full}. Each named
scheme is obtained by naming $(A,\kappa)$: split conformal uses
$A(x,y) = |y - \widehat\mu(x)|$ with one stratum; locally weighted or normalized
conformal uses $A(x,y) = |y - \widehat\mu(x)|/\widehat\sigma(x)$; conformalized quantile
regression uses $A(x,y) = \max\{\widehat q_{\mathrm{lo}}(x) - y,\ y - \widehat
q_{\mathrm{hi}}(x)\}$; Mondrian and group-conditional conformal keep the score simple and
make $\kappa$ a fixed partition; the universally consistent conformal predictive system
of \citet{vovk2022universal} uses a fixed dyadic partition $\kappa = \kappa_n$ with cells
shrinking in $n$.

\begin{lemma}[Reshaping form]\label{lem:invariant}
Suppose $A(x,\cdot)$ is a strictly increasing bijection of $\R$ for each $x$. Then the
law $\widehat F_x$ of \eqref{eq:cdf} is the pushforward of the pooled law
$\widehat G_{\kappa(x)}$ through $A(x,\cdot)^{-1}$,
\[
  \widehat F_x \;=\; A(x,\cdot)^{-1} \!\circ\, \widehat G_{\kappa(x)} .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from \eqref{eq:cdf}: $\widehat F_x(y) = \widehat G_{\kappa(x)}(A(x,y))$ is the
distribution function of $A(x,\cdot)^{-1}(W)$ for $W \sim \widehat G_{\kappa(x)}$.
\end{proof}

The content of Lemma~\ref{lem:invariant} is that in every conformal scheme the input $x$
touches the predictive law in exactly two frozen ways: through the deterministic
reshaping $A(x,\cdot)$, and through the choice of which stratum's pooled empirical law
to use. All non-location content of the forecast, its shape, is a single pooled object
$\widehat G_{\kappa(x)}$, common to every $x$ in a stratum.

\section{The excess-risk floor}

\begin{assumption}[Frozen design]\label{as:frozen}
The pair $(A,\kappa)$ is measurable with respect to the training fold and independent
of the calibration labels $\{Y_i\}$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Regularity]\label{as:reg}
For each $x$, $A(x,\cdot)$ is a strictly increasing, absolutely continuous bijection
$\R \to \R$ with derivative $A'(x,y) = \partial_y A(x,y) > 0$. The laws
$P(\cdot\given x)$ and the stratum-pooled score laws admit densities, and the
divergences below are finite.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{as:frozen} is not a modeling choice; it is what the exchangeability
argument requires. If $(A,\kappa)$ were refined using the calibration labels of the
points being predicted, the calibration and test scores would no longer be exchangeable
and the distribution-free guarantee would fail. Sample splitting, in which the design is
fit on one fold and frozen for another, is the standard way to satisfy
Assumption~\ref{as:frozen}, and it is still a frozen design.

Write $W = A(X,Y)$ for the score as a random variable and $\kappa = \kappa(X)$ for the
stratum. Let $P_W(\cdot\given x)$ be the true conditional law of $W$ given $X=x$, and let
$G_\kappa = P_W(\cdot\given \kappa(X)=\kappa)$ be the stratum-pooled score law, the
population object that $\widehat G_\kappa$ estimates.

\begin{theorem}[Irreducible floor]\label{thm:floor}
Under Assumptions~\ref{as:frozen} and \ref{as:reg}, the excess risk of the conformal
predictor $\widehat F_n$ of Definition~\ref{def:conf} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:floor}
  L(\widehat F_n) - H(Y\given X)
  \;=\; \I\big( W ; X \given \kappa \big) \;+\; \Delta_n ,
  \qquad \Delta_n \to 0 ,
\end{equation}
where $\I(W;X\given\kappa) = \E_X\,\KL\!\big(P_W(\cdot\given X)\,\|\,P_W(\cdot\given
\kappa(X))\big)$ is the conditional mutual information between the score and the input
given the stratum, and $\Delta_n = \E_X\,\KL\big(G_{\kappa(X)}\,\|\,\widehat
G_{\kappa(X)}\big)$ is the pooled-density estimation term, which vanishes as the
calibration size in each stratum grows.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix the fold, so $(A,\kappa)$ is deterministic. By Assumption~\ref{as:reg} the score
$W = A(x,Y)$ has, given $X=x$, the density obtained by change of variables,
$p(y\given x) = p_W\big(A(x,y)\given x\big)\, A'(x,y)$. The conformal density is
$\widehat f_x(y) = \widehat g_{\kappa(x)}\big(A(x,y)\big)\, A'(x,y)$ with $\widehat
g_\kappa$ the density of $\widehat G_\kappa$. The Jacobian $A'(x,y)$ is common to both,
so it cancels in the log ratio:
\[
  \log \frac{p(y\given x)}{\widehat f_x(y)}
  \;=\; \log \frac{p_W\big(A(x,y)\given x\big)}{\widehat g_{\kappa(x)}\big(A(x,y)\big)} .
\]
Taking expectations over $Y\given X=x$ and substituting $w = A(x,y)$,
\[
  \KL\big(P(\cdot\given x)\,\|\,\widehat F_x\big)
  \;=\; \KL\big(P_W(\cdot\given x)\,\|\,\widehat G_{\kappa(x)}\big) .
\]
Average over $X$ and use \eqref{eq:excess}. Split the divergence at the population
pooled law $G_{\kappa(x)}$:
\[
  \KL\big(P_W(\cdot\given x)\,\|\,\widehat G_{\kappa(x)}\big)
  = \KL\big(P_W(\cdot\given x)\,\|\,G_{\kappa(x)}\big)
    + \E_{W\sim P_W(\cdot\given x)}\!\left[\log \frac{g_{\kappa(x)}(W)}{\widehat g_{\kappa(x)}(W)}\right] .
\]
The second term averages over $X$ to $\Delta_n$. For the first, because
$\kappa(x)$ is a function of $x$ we have $P_W(\cdot\given X,\kappa)=P_W(\cdot\given X)$
and $G_{\kappa} = P_W(\cdot\given \kappa)$, so
\[
  \E_X\,\KL\big(P_W(\cdot\given X)\,\|\,P_W(\cdot\given \kappa(X))\big)
  \;=\; \I\big(W;X\given\kappa\big)
\]
by the definition of conditional mutual information. This gives \eqref{eq:floor}. The
term $\Delta_n$ is the expected excess of the log-loss of the estimated pooled density
over the true pooled density within a stratum; under Assumption~\ref{as:frozen} the
calibration labels are exchangeable within each stratum, so $\widehat G_\kappa \to
G_\kappa$ and $\Delta_n \to 0$ as each stratum fills.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Positivity and suboptimality]\label{cor:pos}
$\I(W;X\given\kappa) > 0$ unless $W \perp X \given \kappa$, that is, unless the frozen
pair $(A,\kappa)$ renders the score conditionally independent of the input within
strata. When it does not, the excess risk is bounded away from zero for every $n$:
$\liminf_n\,[\,L(\widehat F_n)-H(Y\given X)\,] \ge \I(W;X\given\kappa) > 0$.
\end{corollary}

\section{What calibration cannot do}

The floor $\I(W;X\given\kappa)$ is a functional of the frozen pair $(A,\kappa)$ alone.
The calibration data enter Theorem~\ref{thm:floor} only through $\Delta_n$, which
vanishes. The ranking, quantile, and $p$-value machinery of conformal prediction is
precisely the computation of $\widehat G_\kappa$ and its quantiles, so it moves
$\Delta_n$ and nothing else.

\begin{proposition}[The calibration step is a floor no-op]\label{prop:noop}
Under Assumptions~\ref{as:frozen} and \ref{as:reg}, no choice of calibration estimator,
quantile convention, randomization, or level reduces the excess risk below
$\I(W;X\given\kappa)$. Any reduction of the floor is a change to $A$ or $\kappa$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from \eqref{eq:floor}: the first term does not depend on the calibration
labels once $(A,\kappa)$ is fixed, and every conformal calibration operation is a
function of the pooled scores $\{A(Z_i)\}$, hence acts only through $\widehat G_\kappa$
and $\Delta_n$.
\end{proof}

The reason the floor is positive in practice is sharp enough to state as a remark.

\begin{remark}[The use case is the floor]\label{rem:self}
Call $(A,\kappa)$ \emph{conditionally sufficient} if $W \perp X \given \kappa$. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:pos} the floor is zero exactly when the frozen design is
conditionally sufficient, and a conditionally sufficient design is one whose pooled
residual law already is the conditional law, that is, one for which the base modeling
was finished. Conformal calibration is deployed when one is unwilling to assume the
residual law is conditionally adequate. That unwillingness is the negation of
conditional sufficiency, and by Corollary~\ref{cor:pos} it is the condition under which
the floor is strictly positive. The method's stated reason for existing is its lower
bound.
\end{remark}

\section{Full conformal}\label{sec:full}

In full conformal prediction the score is refit for each candidate, so $A$ depends on
the augmented multiset $\{Z_1,\dots,Z_n,(x,y)\}$ through a function that is symmetric in
its arguments. The reshaping $A(x,\cdot)$ is then random, but it remains measurable with
respect to the data and, crucially, the symmetry is what supplies exchangeability rather
than a separate fold.

\begin{lemma}[Full-conformal floor]\label{lem:full}
Let $A_n$ be a nonconformity score that is symmetric in the calibration points and, for
fixed $x$, strictly increasing and absolutely continuous in $y$, and suppose $A_n(x,\cdot)
\to A_\infty(x,\cdot)$ and the pooled score law converges to a limit $G_{\kappa(x)}$
almost surely. Then the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:floor} holds with $A_\infty$ in
place of $A$: the limiting excess risk is $\I(A_\infty(X,Y);X\given\kappa)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Condition on the calibration multiset. Symmetry makes the augmented scores exchangeable,
so the rank of the test score is uniform and \eqref{eq:cdf} holds with the realized
$A_n$. The change of variables in Theorem~\ref{thm:floor} uses only that $A_n(x,\cdot)$
is a strictly increasing absolutely continuous bijection for fixed $x$, which holds
pathwise; the Jacobian cancels as before. Passing to the limit with $A_n \to A_\infty$
and $\widehat G_{\kappa} \to G_\kappa$, dominated by Assumption~\ref{as:reg}, gives the
stated floor. The only new ingredient beyond Theorem~\ref{thm:floor} is that the frozen
design is replaced by a data-measurable but label-symmetric design, which preserves both
the reshaping form and the cancellation.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:full} closes the characterization: whether the design is fold-frozen or
refit-symmetric, the predictive is a reshaping of a pooled law and the floor is a
conditional mutual information of the score.

\section{The rate handicap of consistent schemes}

Some schemes attack the floor directly by refining the stratification. The universally
consistent predictive system of \citet{vovk2022universal} takes $\kappa = \kappa_n$ to
be a dyadic partition with cells of width $h_n \to 0$, so that $\I(W;X\given\kappa_n) \to
0$ and the excess risk vanishes. Consistency is genuine. Efficiency is not.

\begin{corollary}[Regressogram handicap]\label{cor:rate}
Let $\kappa_n$ be a data-independent refinement with $|\cK_n| \to \infty$. Then
Assumption~\ref{as:frozen} requires each cell to retain growing calibration mass,
$n/|\cK_n| \to \infty$, for $\Delta_n \to 0$, and forbids choosing the cells from the
calibration labels. The resulting estimator is a fixed-design, label-blind
regressogram, whose excess risk decays at the fixed-design nonparametric minimax rate.
Where the conditional law has spatially inhomogeneous complexity, this rate is strictly
dominated by the label-adaptive minimax rate attained by an estimator permitted to place
resolution using the labels \citep{stone1982optimal,donoho1998minimax}.
\end{corollary}

The handicap has the same cause as the floor. Assumption~\ref{as:frozen} says the
labels may not choose where resolution is spent, so the partition cannot be refined
where the truth is rough and coarsened where it is smooth. A fixed partition pays the
worst-case local complexity everywhere. The consistent conformal scheme is a
regressogram by construction, and a regressogram is minimax-suboptimal wherever
adaptation would help.

\section{Discussion}

The two frozen ingredients $(A,\kappa)$ are a coarsening of the input. Everything a
conformal scheme can represent about the conditional law lives in that coarsening; the
calibration step estimates the pooled score law within it and nothing more. The excess
risk is therefore governed by a single quantity, the conditional mutual information
$\I(W;X\given\kappa)$ that the coarsening leaves behind, and this quantity is fixed
before any calibration is done. Three consequences follow, and together they exhaust the
options.

If the coarsening carries no input dependence in the shape, one stratum and a location
score, the floor is the plain residual information gap $\I(R;X)$
\citep{cotton2026gap}, and no data reduce it. If the coarsening uses the input but is
frozen, normalized, quantile, or Mondrian, the floor is the smaller but still positive
$\I(W;X\given\kappa)$, positive by Remark~\ref{rem:self} exactly when the modeling was
not finished. If the coarsening refines toward sufficiency, the labels may not steer the
refinement, and Corollary~\ref{cor:rate} charges the regressogram rate. In each case the
conformal calibration step contributes zero to the floor, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:noop}. What reduces the floor is a better $(A,\kappa)$, which is
ordinary conditional modeling done before the conformal step, and which the change of
variables shows the conformal step then merely reshapes and certifies.

This does not diminish the coverage certificate, which is exact at its position and
distribution-free. It locates it. The certificate is a statement about the marginal
rank of the test score; the floor is a statement about the conditional information the
frozen score discards. The two live on different axes, and the first cannot move the
second, which is why a valid conformal predictor and a sharp one are related only
through the modeling that precedes the calibration.
